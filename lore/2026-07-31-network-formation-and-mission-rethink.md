# How networks are born — and second thoughts

*Diary entry, 31 July 2026*

This entry records a fast week: a session with Ed and James on network formation, a site restructure plan, a research sprint on inspirations, and then the moment where I stopped and questioned the formulation itself.

---

## The session with Ed and James (Friday 24 July)

We talked about how networks are born. Their model, roughly:

You don't start a network by building an institution. You start with an idea and a specific brand — a style, a taste — and you put it into the world. People recognise something in it, show up, and you organically start building things together. The network forms around the sensibility, not around an org chart.

The homework that came out of it:

1. **Really think about SB's mission.** Get it to something I can stand behind and say in one breath.
2. **Find the example projects we're inspired by.** A constellation of existing work explains the idea better than a definition can — and it signals the taste that people would be showing up for.

---

## The plan that followed: rework the site

I sketched a new structure for the website, built around that logic — mission first, then the why, then the constellation, then our own projects and events.

**Working mission draft:** *Sparkle Bureaucracy is a network of people using AI to explore optimistic organisational and service futures.*

The structure:

- **Mission statement** + newsletter sign-up
- **Why this mission** — we are doing this, for this reason, at this time, and we are inspired by these projects
  - **Organisations we're inspired by**
  - **Projects we're inspired by** — e.g. *Papers, Please* (Lucas Pope, 2013): you play the border officer; documents, stamps, discrepancies, quotas, and a family to feed. It preserves the ritual of border crossing but affects the audience in a way that implies these processes are not humane — it puts you in the seat of power. Also *Cart Life*.
  - **Projects that do inspiring work, but lack sparkle** — Document Assembly Line / Court Forms Online / Spot (Suffolk LIT Lab); genagents (Stanford, Park et al. 2024); Pathfinder (Mobile Pathways); Talk to the City (AI Objectives Institute)
- **Projects** — campaign factory, timetable, Sparkle Border Authority, Project Mirror ("I built you a digital twin!")
  - With an **inclusion rubric**: (1) use of emerging technology (AI); (2) bureaucratic ritual or process; (3) designed affect — how did people feel; (4) was it sparkly?; (5) SB's stated boost for under-resourced civic contexts
- **Events, past and upcoming** — Lumina House takeover (community crossover), Cohort Reunion: Spy Edition (networking as game), Birthday Passports
- **About** — the dictator

## The research sprint (30 July)

To fill the "inspired by" sections with real evidence rather than vibes, I ran a desk-research sprint. The full output is in [`research-longlist/`](./research-longlist/) — method, calibration, four linked longlists of 25 (projects, organisations, directories/archives, people/networks), deep evidence profiles across three lanes, a movements-and-tactics companion lane anchored on Mexico's Glitter Revolution and Portland's inflatable anti-ICE protesters, and a proposed website portfolio in [`website-selection.md`](./research-longlist/website-selection.md). The change record is in [`docs/execution-journal/2026-07-30-sparkle-longlist-research.md`](./docs/execution-journal/2026-07-30-sparkle-longlist-research.md).

The provisional definition the research landed on:

> Sparkle is the designed charge in a consequential procedure: rules and power become possible to feel and understand, people gain meaningful room to participate, question, or act, and the encounter leaves behind civic capability rather than a decorative veneer.

Findings I want to keep regardless of where the framing goes: affect is broader than delight (relief, dignity, moral discomfort, certainty, empathy, productive disagreement all count); status and acknowledgement are affective infrastructure; sparkle may *remove* a ritual rather than decorate it; technology often works best behind a trusted person, not as the sole civic interface; and the clearest warning pattern is **affective legitimacy without recourse** — a friendly process that leaves power untouched and unchallengeable.

The research also left [seven questions it says only I can answer](./research-longlist/website-selection.md#questions-requiring-a-founder-decision) — whether emerging technology is part of Sparkle's definition or just SB's current arena, whether discomfort and grief can count as sparkle, whether power must be transferred or only made legible, and so on.

## Second thoughts (31 July)

After sitting with all of this for a week, I'm not comfortable taking this formulation forward as a movement/project/network. I can't fully articulate why yet — that articulation is the next piece of work. But the discomfort is real enough that I don't want to build the site on top of it as-is.

So today I sat down with Hannah to go a level deeper than mission statements: what do I actually care about, and what kind of impact do I want to have? We put together some ideas — recorded in [What I actually care about](./2026-07-31-what-i-care-about.md). The weekend reflection that explains the discomfort itself is in [The resistance is information](./2026-07-26-the-resistance-is-information.md).

**Status:** the site restructure is paused until the mission question is resolved. The research stands either way — the longlist doesn't stop being useful because the frame is shifting, and the seven founder questions are probably part of *why* the frame is shifting.
