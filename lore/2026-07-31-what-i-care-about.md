# What I actually care about

*Diary entry, 31 July 2026 — an hour with Hannah, then a follow-on chat with Peyman. Continues from [The resistance is information](./2026-07-26-the-resistance-is-information.md).*

I brought Hannah the whole tangle: how to *own* Sparkle Bureaucracy in a way that feels legitimate and exciting rather than scary and overwhelming. What we found: there isn't one thing I care about — there are several, and Ed found a 50,000-feet way of encapsulating them. But that encapsulation isn't in the format I'd want, because it has **no clear problem statement**. So we built one.

## Three registers of caring

- **The fun thing:** playful games that encourage you to think about how the state runs — the birthday border control, the cohort reunion, the pirate ship.
- **The temporary opportunity:** political and civic groups have an AI upskilling gap, especially around agents — and it's closing on someone else's terms if we don't act. CHEW Festival / Project Mirror and Campaign Factory live here.
- **The long-term goal / career:** gov tech.

These don't need collapsing into one thing. The fun thing is the *method*, the opportunity is the *audience and moment*, gov tech is the *horizon*.

## A problem statement, finally

We are a network of people using AI to explore optimistic futures.

1. **The state is failing in many countries.**
2. **AI offers an opportunity to fix this.**
3. **We as technologists need to make sure people working at the grassroots are not left behind.**
4. **We can use AI to prototype and imagine a different future.**

Candidate flagship projects: reimagining grant-making · Campaign Factory · a game.

## A rhythm (determined by the community?)

- **Event 1 — hackathon:** an agents hackathon to prototype a different future, with suggested topics/challenges.
- **Event 2 — demo night:** projects from the hackathon plus anyone else who has one.
- **2× socials / game nights a year:** build an experience — Xmas, Summer.
- **Hear from experts:** a particular domain each time — Andreas, others; new things come out, people teach them.
- **Problem discovery (?):** bring in people *with problems* — not technologists — cross-pollinate, coffee chats.
- **An organiser** to hold the rhythm.

## Scope

**In:** redesigning refugee services · mapping all the community centres in London · election disinformation · gov tech · Alex Vince · Tech for Palestine · Together Against Hate · trade unions · against authoritarianism.

**Out:** working for a political party or one side winning an election · Reform watch.

## The two questions

The whole upskilling thread reduces to a pair of questions anyone can ask: **"What can I use AI for?"** and **"What can AI do for me?"**

## Peyman's nudge: a tangible project

Afterwards I tried to explain to Peyman where the prototype stands, and how I'm struggling to articulate the movement without a tangible project. His idea: if the thing I care about is closing the gap between people who use AI and people who don't know how — build something like **howcaniuseai.com**: it takes information about your tasks and suggests automations. My variant: **an agent that tracks your RescueTime-equivalent, studies your patterns, and suggests tools, workflows, skills, and plugins** — an upskilling diagnostic that meets people inside their actual work.

That would be an artifact between me and the audience — the thing the [Sunday reflection](./2026-07-26-the-resistance-is-information.md) said I needed. Judge the thing I made.
