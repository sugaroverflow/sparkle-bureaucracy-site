# Discovery: affective bureaucracy and organisational rituals

Desk research completed 30 July 2026. This is a discovery list, not a final endorsement list. It covers:

- **Lane 1:** games, installations, performances and other works that make bureaucracy emotionally legible;
- **Lane 5:** redesigned rituals of permission, hiring, evaluation, grants, budgets and workplace governance.

Primary or official project sources are used for project mechanics and status. Affect is separately labelled as **maker-claimed**, **observed** by a critic/researcher, **participant-reported**, or **inferred by us**. An inference is not treated as evidence.

## Fast editorial read

The strongest website candidates from these two lanes are:

1. **Papers, Please** — the clearest demonstration that an administrative interface can make the moral consequences of rules felt.
2. **New World Summit** — changes the architecture and authorised participants of parliament, not only its visual style.
3. **Transborder Immigrant Tool** — an unusually literal combination of emerging technology, poetry and survival infrastructure, but its real-world use needs careful verification.
4. **Open Hiring** — a legible process transformation: remove the audition and move support to after entry.
5. **Cobudget** — software plus a social ritual that shares power over money, with unusually good experience evidence.
6. **Buurtzorg** — purpose-built technology supporting autonomous teams and warmer public care, with strong outcome evidence.
7. **HRC Explorer Grants** — makes uncertainty explicit by using a qualified lottery; an excellent procedural reference even though its affect is not designed.

**The List**, **Border Tuner**, **Cart Life**, and **I Get This Call Every Day** are strong Sparkle ancestors: they make systems felt, but do not themselves redesign a live civic service. **FundAction**, **Edge Fund**, and **Red Umbrella Fund** are especially important sources on power transfer, even where their technology or affective staging is modest.

## Project candidates

### 1. Papers, Please

- **Dates/status:** Released 2013; commercially available on desktop, PlayStation Vita and mobile.
- **Geography/community:** Fictional Arstotzka; draws on Eastern European border-control imagery. Global player audience.
- **Organisation / people / network:** 3909 LLC; designed, developed and published by Lucas Pope.
- **Public URL:** [official site](https://www.papersplea.se/) and [official press kit](https://www.papersplea.se/presskit/).
- **Discovery source:** Sparkle Bureaucracy starter case; corroborated through the official project and retrospective criticism.
- **What it does:** Places the player behind an immigration inspection desk. The player compares passports, permits and changing rulebooks while meeting quotas and paying for their family’s food, heat and medicine.
- **Bureaucratic ritual/process:** Border intake, document verification, eligibility classification, discretionary decision, stamp, detention or refusal.
- **Technology and its actual role:** A digital simulation is constitutive: the interface makes repeated classification, time pressure and branching consequences playable. It is not AI.
- **Intended affect:** Moral pressure, complicity and conflict between procedural correctness, compassion and survival.
- **Evidence of experienced affect:**
  - **Observed:** Contemporary and retrospective critics describe responsibility, remorse, tension and the unsettling experience of becoming the official whose small actions enact distant rules. [PopMatters](https://www.popmatters.com/184010-the-bureacracy-of-play-2495635987.html) and [Game Informer’s ten-year retrospective](https://gameinformer.com/2024/01/02/papers-please-10-years-later).
  - **Participant-reported:** Not systematically evaluated in the sources reviewed. The official press kit collates reviews rather than player research.
- **Civic problem / beneficiary:** It does not deliver a service. Its civic value is public reflection on borders, discretion and the human cost of rule-following.
- **Where power sits / contestability:** The state changes rules; the player has constrained discretion and can sometimes resist at personal cost. Entrants have almost no formal recourse. This asymmetry is the point.
- **Durable artifact or capability:** The game, rule/interface grammar, 20 endings and a large body of educational and critical use.
- **Classification:** **Sparkle ancestor.** Strong ritual, technology and affect; indirect rather than operational civic capacity.
- **What SB borrows:** Put the audience in the consequential administrative role; make changing policy arrive as mundane interface updates; connect each stamp to a human consequence.
- **Concern/gap:** It risks centring the official’s moral experience over the migrant’s agency. It diagnoses bureaucracy rather than improving it.
- **Evidence confidence:** **High** for mechanics and critical reception; **medium** for claims about player affect because no formal experience study was found.
- **Website readiness:** **Ready.** A concise, compelling anchor case, provided the limitation above is stated.

### 2. Cart Life

- **Dates/status:** First released 2010; expanded commercial release in 2013; code and game released freely in 2014. A planned remake was announced in 2023 and shelved in 2024. The original survives in source mirrors and web archives. [Wired history](https://www.wired.com/story/mystery-of-cart-life-death-rebirth/) and [archived/open-source status](https://steamcommunity.com/app/233390/discussions/0/540736781051386131/).
- **Geography/community:** Fictional US city; focused on street vendors, precarious workers, migrants and a single parent.
- **Organisation / people / network:** Independent creator Richard Hofmeier; later AdHoc Studio was involved in the abandoned remake.
- **Public URL:** [Internet Archive copy](https://archive.org/details/cart-life-v1.6); the former creator page is no longer a dependable project source.
- **Discovery source:** Sparkle Bureaucracy starter case; Games criticism on labour and empathy.
- **What it does:** Simulates the working and domestic lives of three street vendors. Repetitive tasks, stock, permits, prices, sleep and relationships compete for limited time.
- **Bureaucratic ritual/process:** Small-business licensing and compliance, vending routines, inventory, rent and the timetable of precarious work.
- **Technology and its actual role:** A low-resolution digital simulation built in Adventure Game Studio; repetition and limited time make systemic pressure experiential. No AI.
- **Intended affect:** Hofmeier framed the work partly as a humorous exploration of normality, but its systems produce exhaustion, frustration, tenderness and empathy.
- **Evidence of experienced affect:**
  - **Observed:** Critics repeatedly describe it as an affecting view from inside poverty-line work and as an empathy-producing study of everyday struggle. [Wired on role-play and empathy](https://www.wired.com/story/gaming-role-play-empathy/) and [GameSpot’s “The Empathy Game”](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/the-empathy-game/1100-6402148/).
  - **Participant-reported:** No structured player evidence found.
- **Civic problem / beneficiary:** Makes precarious urban labour and the administrative load on tiny businesses visible; no direct service beneficiary.
- **Where power sits / contestability:** Vendors absorb risk from markets, time and regulation. Players can optimise but cannot meaningfully contest the system.
- **Durable artifact or capability:** A historically important game and released source code, although preservation and official availability are messy.
- **Classification:** **Sparkle ancestor.** Strong affective systems design; no live civic boost or emerging technology.
- **What SB borrows:** Treat time, repetition and tiny operational choices as emotional material; show the “administrative load” across work and home rather than as a single form.
- **Concern/gap:** The website must not claim current support or a successful 2023 rerelease. The game reveals constraint without transferring power.
- **Evidence confidence:** **High** for mechanics/history; **medium** for intended and experienced affect.
- **Website readiness:** **Ready with preservation note.**

### 3. I Get This Call Every Day

- **Dates/status:** Original release 2012; later Steam release in 2016; the store listing now appears delisted although its metadata remains. [SteamDB record](https://steamdb.info/app/291090/info/).
- **Geography/community:** Canada; based on creator David S. Gallant’s experience in a Canada Revenue Agency call centre.
- **Organisation / people / network:** Independent creator David S. Gallant; voice work by Gallant and music by Pete Mascio.
- **Public URL:** [Steam community archive](https://steamcommunity.com/app/291090/) and [GameSpot project page](https://www.gamespot.com/games/i-get-this-call-every-day/).
- **Discovery source:** Games writing about empathy, invisible service work and procedural frustration.
- **What it does:** Recreates one difficult taxpayer call. The player chooses scripted responses while trying to satisfy confidentiality rules, an impatient caller and employer expectations. Most paths end in dismissal.
- **Bureaucratic ritual/process:** Public-service call handling, identity verification, confidentiality, scripts, escalation and performance discipline.
- **Technology and its actual role:** Short point-and-click simulation; technology renders the script and narrow response menu visible. No AI.
- **Intended affect:** Frustration, claustrophobia and recognition of how little discretion a frontline worker has.
- **Evidence of experienced affect:**
  - **Observed:** GameSpot argues that the short experience opens a window into the worker’s position and may produce empathy for the person on the other end of a service call. [The Empathy Game](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/the-empathy-game/1100-6402148/).
  - **Maker-reported consequence:** Gallant was dismissed after press connected the fictionalised game to his employment, demonstrating the institutional risk of making invisible labour legible. [Yahoo News Canada](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/blogs/right-click/revenue-canada-employee-fired-over-customer-video-game-190145577.html).
  - **Participant-reported:** No systematic player evidence found.
- **Civic problem / beneficiary:** Makes the emotional labour and conflicting rules of public contact centres legible. It offers no service improvement.
- **Where power sits / contestability:** The caller and employee are both constrained, but the organisation owns the script, confidentiality standard and employment sanction. Almost no in-game appeal exists.
- **Durable artifact or capability:** A compact playable procedural argument; availability is fragile.
- **Classification:** **Sparkle ancestor.** Affectively exact, technologically modest, diagnostic rather than capacity-building.
- **What SB borrows:** One ordinary transaction can expose an entire institution; script limits are a design material; include the frontline worker as a subject of bureaucratic harm.
- **Concern/gap:** The caller is deliberately irritating, which may displace criticism from the institution onto the public. Current legal availability should be checked before linking readers to a download.
- **Evidence confidence:** **High** for provenance and mechanics; **medium** for audience affect.
- **Website readiness:** **Reserve / ready after link verification.**

### 4. Orwell: Keeping an Eye on You

- **Dates/status:** Released episodically in 2016; currently sold for PC, Mac, iOS and Android.
- **Geography/community:** Osmotic Studios is based in Germany; the setting is a fictional state called The Nation.
- **Organisation / people / network:** Osmotic Studios; published originally by Surprise Attack/Fellow Traveller.
- **Public URL:** [official project page](https://www.osmoticstudios.com/orwell-keeping-an-eye-on-you/).
- **Discovery source:** Games about administrative complicity and technology-mediated classification.
- **What it does:** Casts the player as a human analyst inside a state surveillance platform. The player reads citizens’ messages, files and public posts, then chooses which fragments to upload to security services.
- **Bureaucratic ritual/process:** Intelligence intake, data matching, profiling, evidence selection and escalation.
- **Technology and its actual role:** A fictional large-scale surveillance system plus a real desktop-game interface. The key design move is keeping a human classifier inside the automated system. No generative AI.
- **Intended affect:** Suspicion, moral unease and uncertainty about privacy, security and decontextualised evidence.
- **Evidence of experienced affect:**
  - **Maker-claimed:** The official page poses the costs of misinterpretation and asks what the player’s disclosures do to citizens.
  - **Observed:** GameSpot describes destroying a person through selectively supplied information as deliberately disturbing; Game Developer notes the chilling consequences of apparently simple clicks. [GameSpot review](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/orwell-ignorance-is-strength-review-staunch-survei/1900-6416888/) and [Game Developer analysis](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/believing-is-seeing-i-orwell-i-and-surveillance-sims).
  - **Participant-reported:** No systematic study found.
- **Civic problem / beneficiary:** Public understanding of surveillance, context collapse and human responsibility in automated systems; no direct beneficiary.
- **Where power sits / contestability:** The analyst chooses data, but security institutions decide consequences; surveilled citizens cannot inspect or appeal the profile.
- **Durable artifact or capability:** Two released games, localisation resources and a repeatable “human-in-the-loop” narrative form.
- **Classification:** **Sparkle ancestor.** Strong emerging-system critique and affect, but no civic capacity boost.
- **What SB borrows:** Make the supposedly neutral human-in-the-loop visible; show how a database field becomes an irreversible institutional action.
- **Concern/gap:** Player choice is constrained by narrative requirements. The fictional platform can make surveillance thrilling while offering those surveilled no agency.
- **Evidence confidence:** **High** for mechanics; **medium-high** for observed affect.
- **Website readiness:** **Ready**, especially as a critical rather than optimistic ancestor.

### 5. New World Summit / People’s Parliament of Rojava

- **Dates/status:** New World Summit founded in 2012; temporary summits followed by a permanent public parliament opened in Derîk/Rojava in 2018. The project remains documented and the building is described as a site of day-to-day assembly. [Van Abbemuseum collection record](https://vanabbemuseum.nl/en/collection/new-world-summit-rojava).
- **Geography/community:** Berlin, Brussels, Utrecht, Rojava/northern Syria and other sites; stateless, autonomist and blacklisted political movements.
- **Organisation / people / network:** Studio Jonas Staal / New World Summit, with political movements including the Democratic Self-Administration of Rojava; architecture by Paul Kuipers and extensive movement partners.
- **Public URL:** [official Utrecht summit record](https://www.jonasstaal.nl/projects/new-world-summit-utrecht/) and [Cooper Hewitt overview](https://exhibitions.cooperhewitt.org/designing-peace/new-world-summits/).
- **Discovery source:** Arte Útil; art, assembly and alternative-institution research.
- **What it does:** Builds alternative parliaments with organisations excluded from dominant democratic forums. Spatial design, symbols, seating, translation and agendas are developed to materialise other forms of political representation.
- **Bureaucratic ritual/process:** Parliamentary authorisation, representation, agenda-setting, speech, translation and diplomatic recognition.
- **Technology and its actual role:** Architecture, livestreaming and documentation support the assembly, but emerging technology is not the main intervention.
- **Intended affect:** Legitimacy, solidarity and the felt possibility that excluded movements are political authors rather than objects of security policy.
- **Evidence of experienced affect:**
  - **Participant-reported / reviewer-participant:** A member of the invited Rojava delegation described first-person accounts as hard-hitting and said the event temporarily displaced helplessness and apathy, while also questioning whether symbolic diplomacy could deliver political leverage. [Temporary Art Review](https://temporaryartreview.com/new-world-embassy-rojava/).
  - **Observed:** The Cooper Hewitt account notes that circular assembly can heighten shared embodiment but can also feel like a wall to late arrivals—useful evidence that affect depends on access and choreography.
- **Civic problem / beneficiary:** Exclusion of stateless and blacklisted movements from democratic representation; beneficiaries are participating movements and publics who encounter their political ideas directly.
- **Where power sits / contestability:** Participants gain agenda and representational space, but Studio Jonas Staal and commissioning institutions retain curatorial power. Critics ask whether the art frame offers genuine leverage or symbolic inclusion.
- **Durable artifact or capability:** A permanent parliament in Rojava, temporary assembly architectures, publications, films, transnational alliances and an organisational method.
- **Classification:** **Sparkle ancestor**, very close to an exemplar: little emerging technology, exceptionally strong ritual redesign, affect and civic capacity.
- **What SB borrows:** Redesign the room and symbols together with who is authorised to speak; let a speculative institution become durable infrastructure; treat assembly as organisational art.
- **Concern/gap:** It is politically aligned, not a neutral forum. Website copy should name curatorial power and the difference between visibility, recognition and material political leverage.
- **Evidence confidence:** **High** for form, partners and durable outputs; **medium-high** for affect and political effect.
- **Website readiness:** **Ready.** One of the best cases for explaining that Sparkle can be solemn and institution-building rather than decorative.

### 6. Transborder Immigrant Tool

- **Dates/status:** Developed and field-tested 2007–2012; archived as a prototype and often described as ongoing as an artistic project. No reliable evidence found that it was deployed at scale by migrants.
- **Geography/community:** Mexico–United States desert border; intended for people making dangerous crossings.
- **Organisation / people / network:** Electronic Disturbance Theater 2.0 / b.a.n.g. lab at UC San Diego; Ricardo Dominguez, Brett Stalbaum, Amy Sara Carroll, micha cárdenas, Elle Mehrmand and Jason Najarro.
- **Public URL:** [Arte Útil project record](https://arte-util.org/projects/transborder-immigrant-tool/) and [SIGGRAPH decolonial media-art record](https://decolonial-media-art.siggraph.org/exhibition/piece/the-transborder-immigrant-tool-tbt-la-herramienta-transfronteriza-para-inmigrantes/).
- **Discovery source:** Arte Útil archive.
- **What it does:** Repurposes low-cost GPS phones to point toward water caches and safer routes while delivering a 24-hour sequence of experimental desert-survival poetry—a “Geo Poetic System.”
- **Bureaucratic ritual/process:** It intervenes outside the official border-admission process, countering the geography and information asymmetry created by enforcement rather than redesigning an application workflow.
- **Technology and its actual role:** GPS, low-cost phones, custom code and location-aware audio/text are essential. At its time this was emerging, accessible mobile technology.
- **Intended affect:** Safety, orientation, encouragement and poetic recognition in a system designed around deterrence and invisibility.
- **Evidence of experienced affect:**
  - **Maker-claimed:** The project says poetry provides inspiration alongside survival information. [Arte Útil](https://museumarteutil.net/museumarteutil.net/projects/transborder-immigrant-tool/index.html).
  - **Observed:** The project triggered intense political and institutional reaction, including investigations; that evidences controversy, not migrant experience. [Furtherfield interview](https://www.furtherfield.org/global-positioning-an-interview-with-ricardo-dominguez/).
  - **Participant-reported:** **Not found. Affect not evidenced among intended users.**
- **Civic problem / beneficiary:** Death and disorientation produced by dangerous border crossings; intended beneficiaries are migrants in the desert.
- **Where power sits / contestability:** The tool routes around state authority and contests the border’s lethal information regime. Intended users did not co-govern the project in the evidence reviewed.
- **Durable artifact or capability:** Code, poems, tested prototype, extensive critical documentation and a model of combining utility with poetics.
- **Classification:** **Sparkle exemplar candidate**, subject to a major evidence caveat.
- **What SB borrows:** Put affect inside a functional tool, not around it; use obsolete/cheap technology inventively; challenge an institution by redistributing operational knowledge.
- **Concern/gap:** Do not imply demonstrated life-saving use. Safety, accuracy, consent and surveillance risks are unusually high. It may be more influential as an artwork than as deployed infrastructure.
- **Evidence confidence:** **High** for design/prototype; **low** for user adoption, experienced affect and beneficial outcomes.
- **Website readiness:** **Ready only with explicit prototype caveat.**

### 7. The List

- **Dates/status:** UNITED for Intercultural Action has compiled the List of Refugee Deaths since 1993; Banu Cennetoğlu has facilitated public presentations since 2007. Ongoing and updated.
- **Geography/community:** Europe and its borders; presented through newspapers, posters and public sites in multiple countries.
- **Organisation / people / network:** Data compiled by the UNITED network; public dissemination facilitated by artist Banu Cennetoğlu with host newspapers, galleries and biennials.
- **Public URL:** [UNITED project](https://unitedfia.org/campaigns/the-missed-refuge/) and [2018 public newspaper version](https://uploads.guim.co.uk/2018/06/19/TheList.pdf).
- **Discovery source:** Contemporary-art research on documents, migration and memorialisation.
- **What it does:** Turns an accumulating administrative-style dataset—name when known, date, place and reported cause of death—into a public document too large to apprehend casually.
- **Bureaucratic ritual/process:** Recording, classification, publication and public accounting of deaths that official border narratives marginalise.
- **Technology and its actual role:** Research databases and desktop publishing maintain the record, but print scale, placement and duration create the experience. No emerging technology.
- **Intended affect:** Refusal of abstraction; grief, attention and recognition of deaths as the result of policy rather than an anonymous natural disaster.
- **Evidence of experienced affect:**
  - **Maker-reported:** Cennetoğlu has described being overwhelmed on first encountering the list. [Guardian interview](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2018/jun/20/banu-cennetoglu-interview-turkish-artist-the-list-europe-migrant-crisis).
  - **Observed public reaction:** The 280-metre Liverpool installation was repeatedly damaged and ultimately destroyed, evidence of hostility and contestation rather than a representative audience response. [Guardian commentary](https://www.theguardian.com/culture/2018/aug/16/refugee-list-culture-war-banu-cennetoglu).
  - **Participant-reported:** No systematic visitor evidence found.
- **Civic problem / beneficiary:** Public erasure and fragmented documentation of people killed by restrictive border regimes; beneficiaries include families, advocates and publics seeking accountability.
- **Where power sits / contestability:** UNITED determines evidence standards; host organisations and Cennetoğlu control dissemination; the work contests states’ narrative power but offers no direct administrative appeal.
- **Durable artifact or capability:** A continuously maintained record and reproducible publishing method.
- **Classification:** **Sparkle ancestor.** Bureaucratic form becomes a memorial and accusation; no direct service redesign or emerging technology.
- **What SB borrows:** Let accumulation and documentary form carry affect; publish what institutions leave dispersed; resist decorative treatment of serious data.
- **Concern/gap:** The ethics of displaying identifiable deaths and the burden on affected communities need care. Public vandalism must not be represented as universal audience response.
- **Evidence confidence:** **High** for process and public history; **medium-low** for experienced affect.
- **Website readiness:** **Ready**, if described as a counter-record rather than a service project.

### 8. Border Tuner / Sintonizador Fronterizo

- **Dates/status:** Twelve-night participatory installation in November 2019; a major bilingual documentation book was published in 2026.
- **Geography/community:** El Paso, Texas and Ciudad Juárez, Chihuahua; border residents and cultural organisations on both sides.
- **Organisation / people / network:** Rafael Lozano-Hemmer / Antimodular Research; Rubin Center at UTEP, El Paso Community Foundation, Fundación Comunitaria de la Frontera Norte and a large binational production network.
- **Public URL:** [official project and book site](https://www.bordertuner.net/book) and [official credits](https://www.bordertuner.net/credits-and-contacts).
- **Discovery source:** Public-art and border-art research.
- **What it does:** Voice-controlled searchlights create visible bridges of light across the border; when beams align, they open a live audio channel. The installation hosted public conversations and performances.
- **Bureaucratic ritual/process:** It does not change border processing. It creates a temporary public counter-ritual to the border’s normal rituals of inspection, separation and state-to-state communication.
- **Technology and its actual role:** Networked searchlights, directional controls, radio/audio systems and custom software are essential to making connection visible and reciprocal.
- **Intended affect:** Intimacy, visibility, interdependence and shared ownership of a border usually narrated through threat.
- **Evidence of experienced affect:**
  - **Observed:** Tens of thousands gathered, according to the official documentation. Borderlore described exchanges ranging from mundane to meaningful and saw the work as revealing daily humanity across state violence. [Borderlore](https://borderlore.org/border-tuner/).
  - **Participant-reported:** The reviewed sources document speech and participation but do not provide a systematic experience study.
- **Civic problem / beneficiary:** Cross-border estrangement and the exclusion of border residents’ voices from national narratives; beneficiaries were participating residents and community partners.
- **Where power sits / contestability:** Participants controlled beams and speech within a curated event. State border power remained unchanged; funders, artist and organisers controlled infrastructure and programme.
- **Durable artifact or capability:** Bilingual book, recordings, technical knowledge and relationships among binational organisations; the live infrastructure was temporary.
- **Classification:** **Sparkle ancestor**, with exemplar-level technology and affect but weak bureaucratic process change.
- **What SB borrows:** Make connection physically legible at civic scale; use spectacle as the gateway to reciprocal speech; build with organisations on both sides of an institutional divide.
- **Concern/gap:** Spectacle can imply reconciliation without altering coercive authority. A website card should be precise that it changed the public encounter, not border administration.
- **Evidence confidence:** **High** for design and reach; **medium** for affect; **low** for civic-system change.
- **Website readiness:** **Ready as an ancestor / boundary case.**

### 9. Open Hiring at Greyston Bakery

- **Dates/status:** Practised since Greyston’s founding in 1982 and still used by the bakery. In 2026 Greyston Foundation announced that wider programmes were paused while it refocused on Yonkers employment; the bakery and Open Hiring continue. [Greyston home page](https://www.greyston.org/).
- **Geography/community:** Yonkers, New York, with adoption pilots in other US employers; people excluded by criminal records, homelessness, recovery, limited education or conventional screening.
- **Organisation / people / network:** Greyston Bakery / Greyston Foundation; founder Bernie Glassman; later leaders include Joseph Kenner and the Center for Open Hiring network.
- **Public URL:** [Greyston Open Hiring and baker stories](https://shop.greyston.org/pages/our-mission-open-hiring%C2%AE).
- **Discovery source:** Inclusive-employment and organisational-process research.
- **What it does:** Applicants add a name and contact details to a first-come list. When an entry-level production role opens, the next person is offered paid training without a résumé, interview or background check. Performance is assessed in the job, with wraparound support.
- **Bureaucratic ritual/process:** Recruitment, credential screening, interview, background checking, probation and employee support.
- **Technology and its actual role:** Technology is incidental. The innovation is procedural: replace predictive screening with ordered access, paid trial and support.
- **Intended affect:** Dignity, hope, trust and freedom from repeatedly narrating or defending one’s past.
- **Evidence of experienced affect:**
  - **Participant-reported:** Greyston’s baker profiles report stability, direction, teamwork and love for the job. A worker interviewed by a news outlet described arriving after repeated rejection and being surprised there was no application. [Greyston baker stories](https://shop.greyston.org/pages/our-mission-open-hiring%C2%AE) and [employee interview](https://www.kpax.com/news/national/no-application-needed-open-hiring-catching-on-with-employers).
  - **Maker-claimed:** Greyston describes “dignity of work” as a core aim.
- **Civic problem / beneficiary:** Structural exclusion from employment and its effects on poverty, family stability and re-entry after incarceration.
- **Where power sits / contestability:** The employer still defines job fitness and can end training, but applicants are not judged by biography. Access is transparent and ordered; individual employment decisions are not described as appealable.
- **Durable artifact or capability:** A four-decade operating model, employer implementation materials, paid-training pathway and adoption evidence. Greyston reports adopters reducing time/cost to hire and The Body Shop pilot reducing turnover. [Greyston adoption evidence](https://www.greyston.org/finding-employees-post-covid-is-easier-than-you-think-try-open-hiring/).
- **Classification:** **Sparkle ancestor**, close to an exemplar: exceptional process and affect, no emerging technology.
- **What SB borrows:** Remove the humiliating gate rather than making it friendlier; move resources from prediction/screening into support after entry; make access rules simple enough to explain in one sentence.
- **Concern/gap:** Not literally “any job for anyone”: it applies to suitable entry-level roles, uses a waiting list and includes performance assessment. Replication depends on job design and support infrastructure.
- **Evidence confidence:** **High** for mechanics, longevity and worker testimony; **medium** for wider causal claims.
- **Website readiness:** **Ready.** A powerful positive case without aesthetic sparkle.

### 10. FundAction participatory grantmaking

- **Dates/status:** Developed from 2016; active across Europe in 2026.
- **Geography/community:** Grassroots activists across 29 European countries.
- **Organisation / people / network:** FundAction, created with activists and a group of progressive funders; member network includes peer-panel participants and movement organisations.
- **Public URL:** [FundAction Assembly and community guidelines](https://assembly.fundaction.eu/pages/communityguidelines).
- **Discovery source:** Participatory Grantmakers Community and EDGE Funders.
- **What it does:** Activists form the community that sets priorities and decides funding. Sortition selects community members for peer panels; proposals are assessed with attention to substance rather than grant-writing polish. Funding, learning and community-building are linked.
- **Bureaucratic ritual/process:** Grant application, peer assessment, panel selection, award decision, funder accountability and evaluation.
- **Technology and its actual role:** Online assembly infrastructure (Decidim) supports participation and documentation, but governance design rather than software is central.
- **Intended affect:** Trust, care, solidarity and relief from competitive, high-bureaucracy philanthropy.
- **Evidence of experienced affect:**
  - **Participant-reported:** Member Rose Longhurst contrasts participatory funds with conventional philanthropic “theatre” and describes the responsibility, learning and multilingual difficulty of serving on a randomly selected peer panel. [FundAction member reflection](https://medium.com/@FundAction/a-seat-at-the-table-learnings-from-a-decade-of-being-a-participant-acbb8ca3918a).
  - **Maker-claimed:** FundAction explicitly commits to care practices, low-bureaucracy funding and replacing competition with democratic exchange.
- **Civic problem / beneficiary:** Activists closest to injustice typically lack control over philanthropic resources and must compete through professionalised applications.
- **Where power sits / contestability:** Decision power moves to members and randomly selected peer panels, although donors still control whether and how much to contribute. Community guidelines and governance create internal accountability; a clear applicant appeal route was not found.
- **Durable artifact or capability:** A transnational activist community, online assembly, peer-panel process, grant streams and shared guidelines.
- **Classification:** **Sparkle ancestor / exemplar candidate.** Strong civic boost, process and affect; technology is supportive rather than emerging.
- **What SB borrows:** Use sortition to share evaluative power; design grantmaking as relationship and learning; judge ideas without rewarding professional English or proposal theatre.
- **Concern/gap:** Participation is substantial unpaid or underpaid labour and multilingual deliberation creates unequal burdens. Donor power does not disappear.
- **Evidence confidence:** **High** for current model; **medium** for participant experience and outcomes.
- **Website readiness:** **Ready after a short factual check with the latest annual material.**

### 11. Edge Fund participatory grantmaking

- **Dates/status:** Founded 2012; active, with a 2026 Roots Fund round and grantee-led Funding Working Group.
- **Geography/community:** United Kingdom and Ireland; small grassroots groups addressing structural injustice.
- **Organisation / people / network:** Edge Fund, legally a cooperative/community benefit society; current and past grantees serve as members and decision-makers.
- **Public URL:** [official overview](https://www.edgefund.org.uk/home) and [decision process](https://www.edgefund.org.uk/how_we_make_decisions).
- **Discovery source:** Participatory-grantmaking networks.
- **What it does:** Uses short applications, lived-experience reviewers, community committees and collective decision-making. Recent rounds invite grantee organisations—not only individuals—to revise criteria and assess applications, with compensation for review work.
- **Bureaucratic ritual/process:** Eligibility, application, scoring, prioritisation, award, membership and fund governance.
- **Technology and its actual role:** Ordinary web forms and online meetings support the process; no emerging technology is essential.
- **Intended affect:** Ease, solidarity, legitimacy and recognition of lived experience; funding days have also deliberately included food, music or spoken word.
- **Evidence of experienced affect:**
  - **Participant-reported:** A representative of Sandwell Youth in Action called a 2018 funding day one of the most democratic and consistent distributions they had experienced, emphasising the shared expertise and inspiration of the groups present. [Edge Fund annual review](https://www.edgefund.org.uk/ar_2018_2019).
  - **Maker-claimed:** Edge says it tries to remove application “hoops” and make funding easier.
- **Civic problem / beneficiary:** Conventional philanthropy excludes small, politically challenging and non-professionalised groups; beneficiaries are grassroots organisers with direct experience of the harms addressed.
- **Where power sits / contestability:** Members/grantees set or apply criteria and score applications; staff/core group holds operational and trustee responsibilities. The process is reviewed through applicant/community feedback, but no formal appeal route was found.
- **Durable artifact or capability:** More than a decade of grant rounds, a membership network, documented methods, working groups and influence on other funders.
- **Classification:** **Sparkle ancestor.** Strong ritual, power transfer and participant affect; little emerging technology.
- **What SB borrows:** Turn an award day into a civic gathering; treat lived experience as decision expertise; pay community reviewers; let previous recipients govern later rounds.
- **Concern/gap:** The current model has changed from earlier large “Funding Day” voting, so website copy must describe a specific period or the current process—not blend them.
- **Evidence confidence:** **High** for model/history and one direct participant account; **medium** for present-day affect.
- **Website readiness:** **Ready with version/date specificity.**

### 13. Cobudget

- **Dates/status:** Practices began independently in Enspiral and the Borderland community around 2014; Greaterthan stewarded the open-source tool from 2017; Cobudget and Dreams were brought toward a shared Cobudget Association during 2021–2026. Active open infrastructure.
- **Geography/community:** Originated in Aotearoa New Zealand and Scandinavia; used by distributed organisations, networks, grantmakers and intentional communities internationally.
- **Organisation / people / network:** Cobudget Association; roots in Enspiral, Borderland and Greaterthan. Key stewards include Francesca Pick and Hugi Ásgeirsson.
- **Public URL:** [official Cobudget story](https://cobudget.com/about), [open guide](https://greaterthan.gitbook.io/guide-to-collaborative-funding) and [source code](https://github.com/cobudget/cobudget).
- **Discovery source:** Enspiral and decentralised-organisation practice networks.
- **What it does:** Gives members allocations from a common pot (“buckets”) which they can distribute to proposals. Proposals become funded through visible member commitments rather than a central budget owner.
- **Bureaucratic ritual/process:** Budget proposal, deliberation, allocation, approval, tracking and accountability.
- **Technology and its actual role:** Open-source web software makes allocations, proposals and comments visible across distributed groups. The makers stress that software supports a social practice rather than substituting for it.
- **Intended affect:** Agency, shared responsibility, transparency and concrete participation in money decisions.
- **Evidence of experienced affect:**
  - **Participant-reported:** In a League of Intrapreneurs case, 90% of invited members participated; leaders described the process as emotionally engaging and liberating because responsibility and decision power were shared. [Cobudget case study](https://cobudget.com/resources/collaborative-funding-as-a-tool-for-agency-shared-responsiblity).
  - **Maker-claimed:** Cobudget frames its purpose as changing how power and money move.
- **Civic problem / beneficiary:** Centralised budgets suppress participation and concentrate organisational power; beneficiaries are network members and communities affected by shared funds.
- **Where power sits / contestability:** Control moves from a director/finance team to members with allocations. Round designers still decide pot size, allocation rules and participant eligibility. Proposals can be discussed publicly, but formal appeals depend on the host group.
- **Durable artifact or capability:** Open-source platform, association, facilitation practice, Creative Commons guide and years of cases.
- **Classification:** **Sparkle exemplar.** Technology, a redesigned bureaucratic ritual, participant affect and durable organisational capacity are all present.
- **What SB borrows:** Bind symbolic participation to real money; make authority divisible; pair an interface with a facilitated ritual; show the flow of decisions rather than only the result.
- **Concern/gap:** Equal-looking allocations do not erase differences in confidence, time or social capital. The software was not in constant active development for parts of its history; check current hosting before recommending adoption.
- **Evidence confidence:** **High** for model, history and one organisational experience case; **medium** for broader impact.
- **Website readiness:** **Ready.**

### 14. Happy Money Story

- **Dates/status:** Introduced into Greaterthan project work in 2019 and documented in 2022 as an established internal practice; current use outside the collective is not independently mapped.
- **Geography/community:** Greaterthan is a transnational cooperative/collective; first used in distributed project teams.
- **Organisation / people / network:** Practice originated with Charles Davies and was adapted by Greaterthan partners including Francesca Pick and Tom Nixon.
- **Public URL:** [Greaterthan case study and instructions](https://www.greaterthan.works/resources/happy-money-story-decoupling-remuneration-from-calculation).
- **Discovery source:** Greaterthan’s funding-governance practice archive.
- **What it does:** A team agrees a total project budget, then each person tells a “happy money story”—what payment would feel good and why. The group shares, adjusts and converges on remuneration without reducing each contribution to hours or salary negotiation.
- **Bureaucratic ritual/process:** Project budgeting, remuneration, valuation of labour and pay negotiation.
- **Technology and its actual role:** None beyond remote collaboration tools and published documentation. It is a conversational/game-derived protocol.
- **Intended affect:** Playfulness, trust, candour and liberation from the fiction that pay is objectively calculable.
- **Evidence of experienced affect:**
  - **Participant-reported:** Greaterthan partners describe it as liberating, trust-building and playful, and its continued adoption over two years is practical evidence of internal value. The testimony is hosted by the organisation using the practice, so it is not independent.
  - **Observed:** No external evaluation found.
- **Civic problem / beneficiary:** Invisible labour and opaque or adversarial pay decisions in self-managed organisations; beneficiaries are project collaborators.
- **Where power sits / contestability:** The group collectively negotiates within a fixed budget; facilitation, precarity and interpersonal status still affect whose story carries weight. The method makes disagreement discussable but has no external appeal.
- **Durable artifact or capability:** A named repeatable practice, story template, facilitation knowledge and open case study.
- **Classification:** **Sparkle ancestor.** Exceptionally affective ritual redesign; no emerging technology and limited civic-service scope.
- **What SB borrows:** Let emotion and sufficiency become admissible data in a financial process; use story to surface invisible needs and contributions; make a taboo decision playful without making it trivial.
- **Concern/gap:** Evidence comes from enthusiasts inside one collective. “Happy” framing may pressure people toward harmony or disclosure when a formal pay framework and rights are needed.
- **Evidence confidence:** **Medium** for practice and direct testimony; **low** for transferability and outcomes.
- **Website readiness:** **Reserve / useful compact example.**

### 15. Buurtzorg self-managing care teams

- **Dates/status:** Founded in the Netherlands in 2006/2007; active internationally. By 2025 it had more than 14,000 employees and 900 self-managing teams according to an open-access organisational study.
- **Geography/community:** Began in Almelo, Netherlands; home-care clients, nurses and neighbourhood networks, with international adaptations.
- **Organisation / people / network:** Buurtzorg Nederland; founded by nurse Jos de Blok and colleagues.
- **Public URL:** [official model of care](https://www.buurtzorg.com/about-us/buurtzorgmodel/).
- **Discovery source:** Self-management and public-service organisation research.
- **What it does:** Small neighbourhood nursing teams coordinate client care and run their own work without conventional middle managers. Coaches and a small back office support teams; the model starts from client independence and relationships.
- **Bureaucratic ritual/process:** Care assessment, scheduling, allocation, record-keeping, team decisions, escalation and performance management.
- **Technology and its actual role:** Buurtzorg Web incorporates the Omaha System’s standardised care taxonomy and supports assessment, intervention, outcomes and community-wide learning without reinstating managerial hierarchy.
- **Intended affect:** Professional trust and autonomy for nurses; warm, continuous relationships and control for clients.
- **Evidence of experienced affect:**
  - **Observed / research evidence:** A 2025 organisational study reports very high employee and client satisfaction and repeated “best employer” recognition, while identifying purpose-built ICT and coaches as key enabling structures. [Journal of Organization Design](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41469-024-00184-y).
  - **Maker-claimed:** The model explicitly prioritises warm relationships, social interaction and clients’ control over their lives.
  - **Participant-reported:** No first-person qualitative source was captured in this fast pass.
- **Civic problem / beneficiary:** Fragmented, managerialised home care that reduces continuity and professional discretion; beneficiaries are people receiving care, their networks and nurses.
- **Where power sits / contestability:** Teams gain substantial operating authority; clients are placed at the centre of care planning. National regulation, funding and the central organisation remain powerful; formal client appeal routes were not investigated here.
- **Durable artifact or capability:** A scaled care organisation, purpose-built digital platform, standard operating model, coaches, international adaptations and research literature.
- **Classification:** **Sparkle exemplar.** Technology, organisational-process redesign, evidenced affect and civic-service outcomes align strongly.
- **What SB borrows:** Use technology to remove management layers rather than automate judgement; organise around continuity and relationship; preserve a legible shared taxonomy without centralising every decision.
- **Concern/gap:** International copies sometimes retain team leaders and differ from the Dutch model. Strong satisfaction claims need careful attribution and should not substitute for scrutiny of labour intensity, cost or client appeal.
- **Evidence confidence:** **High** for structure, technology and satisfaction evidence; **medium** for transferability.
- **Website readiness:** **Ready.** A strong real-service exemplar whose sparkle is structural rather than theatrical.

### 16. Health Research Council of New Zealand Explorer Grants

- **Dates/status:** Lottery-based allocation began in 2013 and continues in the current 2026 funding round.
- **Geography/community:** Aotearoa New Zealand; health researchers across disciplines, including Māori and Pacific health.
- **Organisation / people / network:** Health Research Council of New Zealand; evaluation researchers Mengyao Liu, Vernon Choy, Philip Clarke, Adrian Barnett, Tony Blakely and Lucy Pomeroy.
- **Public URL:** [2026 Explorer Grants](https://gateway.hrc.govt.nz/funding/ideas-funding/2026-explorer-grants) and [applicant-experience study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41073-019-0089-z).
- **Discovery source:** Research-on-research and experimental-funder archives.
- **What it does:** Uses a short, anonymised application. A committee checks proposals against transformative, viable and Māori-health criteria without scoring/ranking. All fundable applications are randomly ordered and awards are made until the budget runs out.
- **Bureaucratic ritual/process:** Public research-grant application, expert eligibility review, prioritisation and award.
- **Technology and its actual role:** Ordinary online grant administration and random ordering; no emerging technology is integral. The innovation is bounded randomness and anonymous review.
- **Intended affect:** The official design foregrounds fairness, attention to the idea rather than reputation and honesty about reviewers’ inability to distinguish among equally fundable proposals. Affect is not explicitly designed.
- **Evidence of experienced affect:**
  - **Participant-reported / survey:** In a survey of 126 applicants from 2013–2019, 63% accepted random allocation for this scheme and 25% opposed it; 89% supported applicant anonymity and 91% considered the application format adequate. Support was much higher among winners, showing affect and legitimacy remain outcome-sensitive. [Open-access study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6996170/).
  - **Inferred by us:** The literal lottery may create suspense or relief, but these emotions were not measured and should not be claimed.
- **Civic problem / beneficiary:** Peer-review bias, false precision and burden can suppress unconventional health research; beneficiaries are researchers with risky ideas and, indirectly, the health system/public.
- **Where power sits / contestability:** The committee still decides eligibility/fundability; chance decides among that pool. Anonymity limits prestige bias. No appeal mechanism was examined.
- **Durable artifact or capability:** A recurring government programme, application guidelines, seven-plus rounds of operational data and an applicant survey that supports adaptation by other funders.
- **Classification:** **Infrastructure awaiting Sparkle.** Excellent process redesign and evidence, little designed affect or emerging technology.
- **What SB borrows:** Admit uncertainty instead of disguising it as precise ranking; separate threshold judgement from allocation; make randomness bounded, public and evaluable.
- **Concern/gap:** Randomness is accepted conditionally, and winners are more positive. The Māori-health criterion is substantive rather than random and should not be omitted in simplified descriptions.
- **Evidence confidence:** **High.**
- **Website readiness:** **Ready**, particularly for a “valuable work where sparkle is unproven” section.

## Emerging Sparkle hypotheses from these lanes

These are research hypotheses, not a settled definition:

- Sparkle often appears when a familiar ritual is **kept legible but its authority is reassigned**: applicant becomes reviewer, excluded movement becomes parliamentarian, worker enters before being judged.
- Affect is strongest when it is generated by a **real consequence or constraint**, not added as visual tone: a stamp, a shared pot of money, a random draw, a paid job, a live voice across a border.
- Good technology makes power **inspectable, divisible or reachable**. It is weak when it merely makes an unchanged gate faster or friendlier.
- “Optimistic” does not mean cheerful. Grief, moral discomfort and productive uncertainty can sparkle when they increase attention, agency or solidarity.
- The central anti-pattern is **affective legitimacy without recourse**: a beautiful parliament with no leverage, friendly surveillance, a playful grant process that still hides who sets the pot, or participation built on unpaid labour.
