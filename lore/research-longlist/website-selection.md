# Website Selection

Status: editorial draft from the 25-project longlist
Research date: 30 July 2026

This is a proposed portfolio, not a claim that the selected projects call themselves Sparkle Bureaucracy. The aim is to explain the idea through a constellation of projects that each reveal a different ingredient.

## Definition after the longlist

> **Sparkle is the designed charge in a consequential procedure: rules and power become possible to feel and understand, people gain meaningful room to participate, question, or act, and the encounter leaves behind civic capability rather than a decorative veneer.**

This is still provisional. The longlist currently suggests that emerging technology is Sparkle Bureaucracy's chosen material and historical context, while the deeper quality called Sparkle can also be found in low-tech forms, games, architecture, organisational design, and the deliberate removal of a bureaucratic ritual.

The strongest cross-case findings are:

- Affect is broader than delight. Relief, dignity, moral discomfort, certainty, trust, suspense, empathy and productive disagreement can all be relevant.
- Affect can reside in the presentation itself: visual language, pacing, interaction, ritual and mechanics can make an otherwise inaccessible system felt. Publications, events, surveys and participant testimony are not prerequisites for Sparkle.
- Status is affective infrastructure. Acknowledgement, tracking and closure often matter as much as a friendly front door.
- Sparkle may remove a ritual rather than decorate it.
- Technology often works best behind a trusted person or shared object, not as the sole civic interface.
- Staff relief is useful evidence, but it is not evidence that a resident felt heard.
- The institutional handoff is part of the experience. Invitation without response can reduce trust.
- The clearest warning pattern is affective legitimacy without recourse: a beautiful or friendly process that leaves power untouched and unchallengeable.

## Proposed: 10 projects “we’re inspired by”

### 1. *Papers, Please* — Lucas Pope

**What it is:** A 2013 game that puts the player behind an authoritarian border-inspection desk. **Bureaucracy:** documents, changing rules, quotas, approval, detention and refusal. **What people felt:** critics and games researchers describe moral discomfort, complicity, tension and the pressure to become ruthlessly efficient. **What SB borrows:** policy should arrive as a felt interface, with every mundane stamp connected to a human consequence. **Unresolved:** the migrant has almost no agency; the game diagnoses the institution rather than improving it. [Evidence profile](./discovery-affect-rituals.md#1-papers-please)

### 2. *Models All the Way Down* — Christo Buschek and Jer Thorp

**What it is:** A visual investigation that follows how 5.8 billion image–text pairs became LAION-5B, an influential foundation dataset. **Bureaucratic relationship:** indirect. The dataset is an administrative artifact left by organised acts of crawling, classification, standardisation, threshold-setting and release; it is not itself a public bureaucracy. **What is the affect:** the presentation is the affect—scrolling image grids, accumulation, repetition and abrupt numerical thresholds turn an incomprehensible data supply chain into awe, alarm and suspicion. **What SB borrows:** make scale bodily comprehensible, trace mundane classifications to public consequences, and publish the evidence beneath the experience. **Unresolved:** it reveals and audits the pipeline but does not give people represented in the data a route to appeal or correction. [Evidence profile](./discovery-ai-governance.md#17-models-all-the-way-down)

### 3. New World Summit / People’s Parliament of Rojava — Jonas Staal and partners

**What it is:** Alternative parliaments co-created with political movements excluded from dominant diplomatic forums, including a permanent parliament in Rojava. **Bureaucracy:** authorisation, representation, agenda-setting, translation, speech and institutional space. **What people felt:** participant and observer accounts describe solidarity, embodiment and a temporary displacement of helplessness, while also questioning the limits of symbolic recognition. **What SB borrows:** redesign the room, symbols and authorised participants together; allow speculative administration to harden into infrastructure. **Unresolved:** visibility and legitimacy do not automatically create political leverage, and curatorial power remains. [Evidence profile](./discovery-affect-rituals.md#5-new-world-summit--peoples-parliament-of-rojava)

### 4. Project Re:form — Civilla and Michigan MDHHS

**What it is:** A statewide redesign of the Michigan benefits application and the staff practice around it. **Bureaucracy:** household disclosure, eligibility intake, evidence and caseworker processing. **What people felt:** applicants reported relief and confidence; staff described less-flustered applicants and gaining an extra minute to be human. **What SB borrows:** a form can become a respectful invitation without theatricality or AI, and frontline-worker affect can reinforce the resident experience. **Unresolved:** eligibility power remains with the state, and the evidence is hosted by the project maker. [Evidence profile](./discovery-public-civic.md#1-project-reform)

### 5. JustFix Letter of Complaint

**What it is:** A free tool that helps New York tenants generate and send a formal repair-demand letter. **Bureaucracy:** guided intake, official artifact, certified handoff, status, follow-up and escalation. **What people felt:** participant accounts describe the tool as accessible and capable of producing action where informal requests failed. **What SB borrows:** the full pipeline matters; a person should leave with an artifact that changes how they can approach power. **Unresolved:** a strong letter cannot by itself correct housing scarcity, retaliation or weak enforcement. [Evidence profile](./discovery-public-civic.md#8-justfix-letter-of-complaint)

### 6. Haqdarshak

**What it is:** An Indian entitlement-discovery and application service combining AI-supported matching with trained local agents, many of them women. **Bureaucracy:** scheme discovery, eligibility, documents, application and follow-up. **What people felt:** participant satisfaction and agent-empowerment evidence suggest the hybrid relationship is part of the intervention, not merely distribution. **What SB borrows:** let AI strengthen a trusted neighbour as the civic interface; build local capability and livelihoods alongside service access. **Unresolved:** outcome and affect claims need continued independent scrutiny, and the agent relationship can introduce new forms of mediation and dependence. [Evidence profile](./discovery-public-civic.md#12-haqdarshak)

### 7. OpenCRVS

**What it is:** Open, configurable civil-registration infrastructure for births and deaths, designed for low-connectivity contexts. **Bureaucracy:** local declaration, verification, registration, status updates and receipt of a foundational identity certificate. **What people felt:** implementation evidence includes families appreciating a more streamlined encounter, although qualitative affect evidence remains limited. **What SB borrows:** acknowledgement and status are emotional design; an institution should show that it has seen a person and where their case now sits. **Unresolved:** identity systems can deepen surveillance or exclusion unless correction, consent and recourse remain visible. [Evidence profile](./discovery-public-civic.md#13-opencrvs)

### 8. Buurtzorg

**What it is:** A Dutch home-care organisation built around small, self-managing nursing teams and a shared digital care system. **Bureaucracy:** assessment, scheduling, allocation, record-keeping, team decisions and performance management. **What people felt:** research reports high employee and client satisfaction alongside warmer, more continuous relationships. **What SB borrows:** technology can remove managerial layers and support professional discretion rather than automate judgement; organisational structure itself can be affective design. **Unresolved:** international adaptations vary, and satisfaction does not replace scrutiny of labour intensity, costs or client appeal. [Evidence profile](./discovery-affect-rituals.md#15-buurtzorg-self-managing-care-teams)

### 9. vTaiwan

**What it is:** A Taiwanese process connecting public agenda-setting, pol.is opinion mapping, facilitated deliberation and government response. **Bureaucracy:** issue intake, position mapping, consensus formation, stakeholder meetings and institutional disposition. **What people felt:** published research and participant accounts support the experience of seeing agreement across apparent camps, although participation is not representative by default. **What SB borrows:** make disagreement navigable, separate consensus from popularity, and require a visible response from the institution at the end. **Unresolved:** the process depends on volunteer capacity and political uptake; participation and consensus can still exclude quieter publics. [Evidence profile](./discovery-ai-governance.md#10-vtaiwan)

### 10. Habermas Machine — Google DeepMind and collaborators

**What it is:** A research prototype in which an AI drafts common-ground statements, receives participant criticism and revises them. **Bureaucracy:** individual positions become a mediated, challengeable collective artifact. **What people felt:** participants rated AI-generated statements as clearer, more informative and less biased than human-mediator statements in the published experiments. **What SB borrows:** synthesis should be iterative and contestable; measure perceived clarity and bias instead of declaring neutrality. **Unresolved:** preference for wording is not democratic legitimacy, minority disagreement can be smoothed away, and no public institution is required to act. [Evidence profile](./discovery-ai-governance.md#2-habermas-machine)

## Proposed companion section: movements we're inspired by

Movements should sit beside projects rather than inside the same rubric. Their authorship is collective and their durable artifact may be a replicable tactic, symbol, role or emotional repertoire.

> **Movement Sparkle is an affective civic tactic that makes institutional power possible to feel, gives people an inhabitable way to confront it, and leaves behind a repertoire others can reuse—without disguising the stakes or transferring attention away from those taking the risk.**

### Mexico's Glitter Revolution / #NoMeCuidanMeViolan

Pink glitter turned a material associated with celebration and femininity into an adhesive mark on police authority during protests against sexual violence and institutional impunity. **What SB borrows:** let the material behave politically and mark power rather than merely decorating participants. **Unresolved:** media framing can detach the image from the violence and demands that produced it. [Evidence profile](./movements-deep-research.md#1-glitter-revolution--nomecuidanmeviolan)

### Operation Inflation / Portland's inflatable anti-ICE protesters

Frogs and other oversized animals placed absurd, soft bodies against the armed seriousness of immigration enforcement. Shared costumes made the action into a role and helped make contemporary footage difficult to confuse with old Portland protest imagery. **What SB borrows:** create roles rather than mascots; design the public record as well as the live encounter. **Unresolved:** spectacle can attract attention away from people directly harmed by detention and deportation, while costumed participants still face real physical and legal risk. [Evidence profile](./movements-deep-research.md#2-operation-inflation--portlands-inflatable-anti-ice-protesters)

### LASTESIS / *Un violador en tu camino*

A short, learnable choreography turned sexual violence from an individualised burden into a synchronised accusation of police, courts and the state. Its score travelled through local translation rather than fixed central control. **What SB borrows:** design a protocol people can embody, repeat and alter without losing the institutional target. **Unresolved:** repetition can become a decontextualised cultural event and participation can expose survivors to retaliation. [Evidence profile](./movements-deep-research.md#3-lastesis-un-violador-en-tu-camino)

### Otpor!'s Milošević barrel

Passers-by paid to hit a barrel bearing the Serbian president's face; police could ignore the public ridicule or arrest the object and complete the joke. **What SB borrows:** map the institution's response options and design an encounter in which disproportion becomes self-describing. **Unresolved:** dilemma actions depend on context; repression is not always reputationally costly to the institution or physically tolerable to participants. [Evidence profile](./movements-deep-research.md#4-otpors-milošević-barrel)

### Mothers of Plaza de Mayo

White headscarves made from children's nappies and a weekly circuit of the square turned enforced movement into a counter-procedure for names and cases the dictatorship tried to disappear. **What SB borrows:** when an institution withholds status, use garment, place and cadence to create a public register that returns. **Unresolved:** the language of motherhood can narrow whose grief is legible, and the organisations' later histories cannot be flattened into a single symbol. [Evidence profile](./movements-deep-research.md#5-mothers-of-plaza-de-mayo)

### ACT UP

SILENCE=DEATH, die-ins and the 1988 FDA action joined graphic urgency and grief to deep procedural knowledge about trials, approvals, funding and insurance. **What SB borrows:** attention should lead into counter-expertise capable of contesting the rule, not stop at a memorable interface. **Unresolved:** famous imagery can erase the care, study and internal organisation that made the visible action consequential. [Evidence profile](./movements-deep-research.md#6-act-up-silencedeath-die-ins-and-the-fda-action)

### Women of Liberia Mass Action for Peace

Christian and Muslim women wore white and sat together, then escalated from witness to physically preventing peace negotiators leaving without an agreement. **What SB borrows:** a counter-uniform can hold a coalition together, but it becomes civic capacity through an escalation path into leverage and later participation. **Unresolved:** popular retellings often reduce a complex movement to a “sex strike” and over-credit a single tactic for the peace outcome. [Evidence profile](./movements-deep-research.md#7-women-of-liberia-mass-action-for-peace)

### Hīkoi mō te Tiriti and the parliamentary haka

Hīkoi, waiata and haka brought embodied Māori political protocol into direct contact with the Treaty Principles Bill; in Parliament, Hana-Rāwhiti Maipi-Clarke tore the document before leading the haka. **What SB borrows:** make the contested artifact tangible and bring living protocol into official procedure. **Unresolved:** culturally grounded forms are sources of principles, not free-floating formats an organisation may copy. [Evidence profile](./movements-deep-research.md#8-hīkoi-mō-te-tiriti-and-the-parliamentary-haka)

## Proposed: 6 valuable projects where Sparkle is absent or unproven

### 1. Document Assembly Line / Court Forms Online / LITEfile

An exemplary shared legal infrastructure: guided interviews, assembled forms, e-filing and newer AI assistance help courts and legal-aid groups serve self-represented litigants. It has durable artifacts, open-source maintenance and obvious civic value. What is missing from the public record is the felt experience of litigants themselves: whether the process reduced fear or humiliation, increased agency, or merely made a demanding court ritual technically completable. [Evidence profile](./discovery-public-civic.md#6-document-assembly-line--court-forms-online--litefile)

### 2. Talk to the City

This open LLM tool makes large bodies of testimony explorable without forcing people into survey boxes. Its reports retain links from clusters to underlying source material, which is an important contestability pattern. Yet the strongest affect claim—helping contributors feel heard—is still primarily maker intent. A clear map of public voice can also make the model's omissions appear authoritative, and the final decision handoff varies by deployment. [Evidence profile](./discovery-ai-governance.md#1-talk-to-the-city)

### 3. Pathfinder

Pathfinder converts unstable immigration-court data into alerts and case intelligence for nonprofit attorneys and legal-service providers. Practitioners report greater clarity and operational confidence, which can indirectly benefit clients in high-stakes cases. The missing evidence is the immigrant's own experience and agency: the technology currently sparkles most clearly for the professional intermediary. [Evidence profile](./discovery-public-civic.md#9-pathfinder)

### 4. HRC Explorer Grants

The Health Research Council of New Zealand uses a short anonymous application, threshold review and random allocation among qualified proposals. It is a rigorous and refreshingly honest response to uncertainty and prestige bias, backed by applicant survey evidence. Its affect is not explicitly designed, however, and acceptance of the lottery is strongly influenced by whether an applicant won. [Evidence profile](./discovery-affect-rituals.md#16-health-research-council-of-new-zealand-explorer-grants)

### 5. Clear My Record

Code for America's work helped transform criminal-record clearance from an individual petition into government-initiated bulk relief. It may be Sparkly precisely because a humiliating ritual disappears and the procedural burden moves back to the institution. But the project has little published evidence about whether people knew relief had occurred or how it felt; invisible automation without notice or correction can become another opaque system. [Evidence profile](./discovery-public-civic.md#4-clear-my-record--automatic-record-clearance)

### 6. Helsinki Summer Streets with UrbanistAI

Residents, businesses and planners used generative imagery to turn words and rough drawings into shared street proposals, then discussed and tested their feasibility. The workshop format is visually vivid and plausibly lowers professional-language barriers. The reviewed sources do not publish independent participant testimony or before/after affect measures, so claims about excitement, ownership or empowerment should remain explicitly unproven. [Evidence profile](./discovery-ai-governance.md#5-helsinki-summer-streets-with-urbanistai)

## Questions requiring a founder decision

The desk research cannot settle these by itself:

1. Is emerging technology part of the definition of **Sparkle**, or a programme constraint for **Sparkle Bureaucracy**?
2. Can moral discomfort and grief count as Sparkle, or must the final emotional direction be hopeful?
3. Must a project transfer or contest power, or is making power legible enough?
4. Can the disappearance of a procedure be Sparkle, or must there be a redesigned encounter?
5. Is under-resourced civic use a hard inclusion rule, a scoring boost, or a commitment governing only Sparkle Bureaucracy's own projects?
6. Should the public website distinguish **designed affect** from **evidenced affect**, even if that makes some project cards visibly inconclusive?
7. Should Sparkle Border Authority and Project Mirror appear alongside outside inspirations as calibration anchors, or remain only under “our projects”?
