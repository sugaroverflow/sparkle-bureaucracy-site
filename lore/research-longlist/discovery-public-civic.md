# Discovery: public-service redesign and under-resourced civic capacity

Status: desk-research discovery pool for lanes 2 and 6. Researched 30 July 2026.

This is deliberately broader than the eventual website selection. It contains projects that explain Sparkle, useful infrastructure in which affect is absent or unproven, and tensions that help define the boundary. Project descriptions privilege official or primary sources. Affect labels follow the evidence model in [`README.md`](./README.md); where a source describes usability or outcomes but not feeling, the record says **affect not evidenced**.

## Project records

### 1. Project Re:form

- **Dates/status:** Mid-2010s redesign, piloted and rolled out statewide; the application remains a live institutional capability.
- **Geography/community:** Michigan residents applying for food, healthcare, childcare, cash, and emergency benefits; MDHHS frontline staff.
- **Organisation / people / network:** [Civilla](https://civilla.org/) with the Michigan Department of Health and Human Services; Civilla co-founders Adam and Lena Selzer and a large state implementation network.
- **Public URL / discovery source:** [Project Re:form case study](https://civilla.org/work/project-reform-case-study); discovered through public-service design references and Civilla.
- **What it does:** Replaced a 40-plus-page, 18,000-word application with a modular form 80% shorter, while changing training and implementation around it. Civilla reports that 90% of pilot users applied in under 20 minutes and 90% felt confident completing it alone.
- **Bureaucratic ritual:** Disclosure of household circumstances, eligibility intake, evidence checking, caseworker processing, and benefit determination.
- **Technology's actual role:** Primarily service, form, policy, and implementation design rather than emerging technology. Its importance is precisely that Sparkle can arise from a redesigned paper encounter.
- **Intended affect:** Respect, confidence, care, and room for a human relationship instead of exhaustion and humiliation.
- **Experienced affect:** **Participant-reported.** An applicant said the new form made them feel “relieved” rather than stressed; caseworkers reported that applicants seemed less flustered and that the shorter form gave them “an extra minute to be human.” These are maker-hosted quotations, but they are direct participant accounts. [Source](https://civilla.org/work/project-reform).
- **Civic problem / beneficiary / under-resourced relevance:** Administrative burden blocked low-income residents from vital benefits and consumed frontline capacity. The redesign is used by roughly 2.5 million applicants a year.
- **Power and contestability:** The state still defines eligibility and makes decisions. The project reduces unnecessary extraction and makes the entry point more respectful, but it does not itself add an appeal channel.
- **Durable artifact or capability:** A statewide application, trained staff across 100 offices, federal approvals, policy alignment, and a repeatable implementation method.
- **Classification / SB borrowing:** **Sparkle ancestor / low-tech exemplar.** Borrow the move from “form as extraction” to “form as respectful invitation,” and treat staff affect as part of service quality.
- **Concern/gap:** No emerging technology; evidence is published by the maker rather than an independent evaluator. The underlying eligibility regime remains intact.
- **Evidence confidence / website readiness:** **High / ready.** It is one of the clearest explanations that reduced anxiety and restored human contact can be Sparkle without whimsy.

### 2. Project One Day

- **Dates/status:** Began in 2019; an 800-case pilot was followed by adoption work with local offices. Current official pages describe the capability as developed and implemented, though no recent independent status audit was found.
- **Geography/community:** Michigan benefits applicants and MDHHS staff.
- **Organisation / people / network:** [Civilla](https://civilla.org/) and Michigan MDHHS.
- **Public URL / discovery source:** [Project One Day](https://civilla.org/work/project-one-day) and [project story](https://civilla.org/stories/one-day-michigan); discovered from the Project Re:form trail.
- **What it does:** Redesigns policy and business operations so determinations for large benefits programmes can be made in one day rather than after 20-plus days.
- **Bureaucratic ritual:** Application receipt, verification, interview/contact, missing-information follow-up, staff review, and eligibility determination.
- **Technology's actual role:** Workflow analysis and tracking support; the central innovation is coordinated policy and operational change, not AI.
- **Intended affect:** Stability in crisis, clarity, and renewed trust in the benefits institution.
- **Experienced affect:** **Maker-claimed.** Civilla explicitly frames same-day determination as a way to build trust and prevent crisis, but the reviewed sources do not quote applicants describing how the redesigned process felt. **Experienced affect not evidenced.**
- **Civic problem / beneficiary / under-resourced relevance:** Delayed determinations and missed calls produce procedural denials when people urgently need food or healthcare assistance.
- **Power and contestability:** Staff and the state retain decision authority. The project compresses waiting and removes handoff failure but does not make eligibility rules or appeals more legible.
- **Durable artifact or capability:** A tested one-day process, operational metrics, adoption guidance, and a north-star model that offices can implement.
- **Classification / SB borrowing:** **Infrastructure awaiting Sparkle.** Borrow the emotionally meaningful north-star metric: not “processing efficiency” but “certainty today.”
- **Concern/gap:** Strong service claim, weak affect evidence, limited public detail on long-term statewide outcomes.
- **Evidence confidence / website readiness:** **Medium / reserve.** Useful contrast card beside Re:form, but not yet a lead inspiration.

### 3. GetCalFresh

- **Dates/status:** 2014–July 2025. Code for America sunset the service after supporting the transition to California's permanent BenefitsCal portal; its plain-language, mobile-first and document-upload patterns were incorporated into the successor.
- **Geography/community:** Californians applying for and maintaining SNAP/CalFresh, especially people applying on mobile devices.
- **Organisation / people / network:** [Code for America](https://codeforamerica.org/) with California Department of Social Services and county agencies; the wider social-safety-net design community.
- **Public URL / discovery source:** [Ten-year retrospective](https://codeforamerica.org/news/reflecting-on-10-years-of-getcalfresh/) and [2025 impact report](https://codeforamerica.org/impact/2025/); discovered through the Digital Government Hub and Code for America.
- **What it does:** Created a shorter, plain-language, mobile application assister; supported document upload, reminders, and benefit-maintenance forms. At its peak it accounted for half of all California SNAP applications and supported 6.2 million approved applications and $12.8bn in benefits.
- **Bureaucratic ritual:** Eligibility questions, household disclosure, document verification, interview, submission, renewal, and procedural deadlines.
- **Technology's actual role:** Mobile-first web forms, conditional logic, uploads, messaging, experimentation, analytics, and integrations. It was not AI-native, but it made contemporary consumer interaction patterns available inside a legacy benefits process.
- **Intended affect:** Remove frustration and intimidation; deliver access with agency and dignity.
- **Experienced affect:** **Participant-reported.** A client described a parent who had been intimidated into relying on food banks but applied after learning how easy GetCalFresh was; the client said the work “saves lives.” [Source](https://codeforamerica.org/impact/2020/). Independent online reports also describe the service as streamlined, but they should not be necessary for the website claim.
- **Civic problem / beneficiary / under-resourced relevance:** Food insecurity compounded by complex county-administered application systems and digital barriers.
- **Power and contestability:** Counties retained eligibility and interview authority; the service reduced entry and evidence burden, but a user could still be denied procedurally downstream.
- **Durable artifact or capability:** Product patterns embedded in BenefitsCal, a decade of research, public benefits-enrollment guidance, and institutional partnership practices.
- **Classification / SB borrowing:** **Sparkle exemplar.** Borrow the bridge strategy: create a humane layer that proves a better encounter, then transfer the pattern into state infrastructure.
- **Concern/gap:** A good front door cannot by itself fix interviews, eligibility policy, or caseworker capacity. Its sunset requires careful website wording.
- **Evidence confidence / website readiness:** **High / ready.** A strong case about humane design becoming institutional memory rather than a permanent parallel service.

### 4. Clear My Record / automatic record clearance

- **Dates/status:** Began in 2016 as petition intake; shifted in 2018 toward automatic clearance. California's bulk-clearance implementation is complete, while Code for America's wider automatic-sealing programme remains active.
- **Geography/community:** California and other US states; people eligible to clear criminal records.
- **Organisation / people / network:** [Code for America criminal justice](https://codeforamerica.org/programs/criminal-justice/), district attorneys, courts, state agencies, advocates, and the Clean Slate movement.
- **Public URL / discovery source:** [California success story](https://codeforamerica.org/success-stories/going-from-pilot-to-statewide-record-clearance-in-california/) and [automatic-clearance best practices](https://codeforamerica.org/resources/automatic-record-clearance-best-practices/); discovered through Code for America and OECD OPSI.
- **What it does:** Moved the burden from individuals filing costly petitions to government identifying and clearing eligible records in bulk. The California tools helped reduce, dismiss, or seal 144,000 convictions affecting 113,000 people.
- **Bureaucratic ritual:** Eligibility matching, prosecutorial review, court/state record update, sealing or dismissal, and notification.
- **Technology's actual role:** Open-source bulk data matching and rules automation turned a one-person-at-a-time petition ritual into government-initiated relief.
- **Intended affect:** Relief without humiliation, repeated disclosure, legal expense, or the need to ask the state individually for a right already granted.
- **Experienced affect:** Code for America documents the effects of records on jobs, housing, and mental health, but the reviewed sources do not establish how recipients felt when automatic clearance occurred. **Project affect not evidenced.**
- **Civic problem / beneficiary / under-resourced relevance:** Petition-based systems reached less than 10% of eligible people and imposed the heaviest burden on those with the fewest resources.
- **Power and contestability:** This is a rare redistribution of procedural responsibility: government must act. Prosecutors/courts retain authority over rules and exceptions; meaningful notice and correction mechanisms remain critical.
- **Durable artifact or capability:** Open software, implementation blueprint, policy principles, and a national Clean Slate implementation field.
- **Classification / SB borrowing:** **Sparkle exemplar structurally; affect unproven.** Borrow “invert the application”: ask why a person must perform a ritual at all when the institution already has the data and duty.
- **Concern/gap:** Invisible automation may produce no felt encounter, and errors or lack of notification can make relief difficult to verify. Code for America's [trauma-informed notification research](https://codeforamerica.org/news/rethinking-the-notifications-process/) is an important companion.
- **Evidence confidence / website readiness:** **High on outcomes, low on affect / ready with explicit caveat.** It tests whether Sparkle can be the disappearance of bureaucracy.

### 5. mRelief SNAP enrolment

- **Dates/status:** Founded in 2014; active. It now combines its long-running web/SMS tools with AI-powered enrolment work.
- **Geography/community:** United States; people screening and applying for SNAP, and community organisations assisting them.
- **Organisation / people / network:** [mRelief](https://www.mrelief.com/); associated with founder Rose Afriyie and former executive director Zareena Meyn; food banks, Catholic Charities, governments, and community navigators.
- **Public URL / discovery source:** [SNAP screener and application](https://www.new.mrelief.com/), [application explanation](https://intercom.help/mrelief/en/articles/9592271-how-is-applying-for-snap-through-mrelief-different-than-applying-directly-at-my-state-portal-or-local-office), and [University of Utah evaluation summary](https://digitalgovernmenthub.org/library/simplified-mobile-friendly-snap-application-increases-application-rates/); discovered through the Digital Government Hub.
- **What it does:** Offers a three-minute eligibility screener and, in supported states, a 10–15-minute application, photo/text evidence submission, reminders, and navigator tools. In a Kentucky randomised study, 59% of people directed to mRelief applied versus 32% sent to the state portal.
- **Bureaucratic ritual:** Pre-screening, eligibility disclosure, application, verification documents, interview, and waiting for a decision.
- **Technology's actual role:** Web, SMS, voice, conditional logic, automated one-by-one evidence prompts, CRM support, and current AI partnerships. Humans remain available through partner organisations.
- **Intended affect:** Simplicity, dignity, choice of channel, and freedom from “list paralysis.”
- **Experienced affect:** **Participant-reported.** One user described the scheduled process as “amazing” compared with spending all day at an office. Another user story describes rapid document submission reducing the stress of feeding a family after childbirth. [Sources](https://www.mrelief.com/press/mrelief_civis), [document story](https://www.mrelief.com/blog/documents_submission_in_just_two_days). The RCT evidences behaviour, not emotion.
- **Civic problem / beneficiary / under-resourced relevance:** Low SNAP take-up, stigma, low digital access, confusing portals, and document burden.
- **Power and contestability:** State agencies still determine eligibility. In places without a simplified mRelief application, users are redirected to the state portal; appeal remains outside mRelief.
- **Durable artifact or capability:** Multichannel enrolment tools, partner CRM, evaluated design patterns, and research briefs.
- **Classification / SB borrowing:** **Sparkle exemplar.** Borrow progressive disclosure, channel choice, and automation that begins with the easiest achievable action.
- **Concern/gap:** Geographic capability varies, and maker stories should not be over-generalised. AI's exact current role needs project-level verification before publication.
- **Evidence confidence / website readiness:** **High / ready.** A strong combination of affective intent, direct testimony, and causal evidence of civic uptake.

### 6. Document Assembly Line / Court Forms Online / LITEfile

- **Dates/status:** Pandemic collaboration began in 2020; active open-source community. LITEfile and AI-assisted authoring/e-filing work are in development and testing.
- **Geography/community:** US courts, legal-aid organisations, students, volunteers, and self-represented litigants.
- **Organisation / people / network:** [Suffolk Legal Innovation & Technology Lab](https://suffolklitlab.org/); David Colarusso, Quinten Steenhuis, Sam Glover, courts and legal-aid partners.
- **Public URL / discovery source:** [Document Assembly Line](https://assemblyline.suffolklitlab.org/docs/get_started/), [Court Forms Online](https://courtformsonline.org/), and [LITEfile update](https://suffolklitlab.org/document-assembly-line-q4-2025-update/); starter case and LSC/TIG ecosystem.
- **What it does:** Gives public-interest teams reusable components for guided legal interviews, document generation, court-form discovery, translation, and e-filing. Court Forms Online helps more than 30,000 people a year.
- **Bureaucratic ritual:** Translate a lived problem into a legal issue, choose a form, answer formal questions, generate documents, sign, file, and receive court processing.
- **Technology's actual role:** Open-source docassemble automation; newer tools use AI for issue spotting, document identification, filing-code suggestions, pre-filling, translation and authoring assistance, subject to human review.
- **Intended affect:** Reduce intimidation, make questions respectful and readable, and let people act without a lawyer.
- **Experienced affect:** A documented story describes a tenant using a phone-generated motion to stop an eviction while belongings were being removed, but no direct emotional account was found. **Affect not evidenced.** [Source](https://assemblyline.suffolklitlab.org/docs/volunteer/sign_up/).
- **Civic problem / beneficiary / under-resourced relevance:** The access-to-justice gap for people who cannot obtain representation, and thin technical capacity in courts and legal aid.
- **Power and contestability:** Users gain procedural agency but courts retain decision authority. The tools can support appeals and filings; disclaimers make clear that they are not legal advice.
- **Durable artifact or capability:** MIT-licensed packages, shared question libraries, authoring tools, e-filing infrastructure, training, workshops, volunteers and a maintenance community.
- **Classification / SB borrowing:** **Infrastructure awaiting evidenced Sparkle.** Borrow the open “assembly line” as shared civic capability and its attention to respectful question-writing.
- **Concern/gap:** Affect remains an intention. Tool discovery, legal accuracy, maintenance and the boundary between information and advice remain material risks.
- **Evidence confidence / website readiness:** **High on infrastructure, medium on user outcomes / infrastructure section.** Already covered in calibration; keep as a defining near-match.

### 7. Upsolve bankruptcy tool

- **Dates/status:** Started in 2016; national launch in 2018; active in 2026.
- **Geography/community:** Low-income and working-class US households eligible for straightforward Chapter 7 bankruptcy.
- **Organisation / people / network:** [Upsolve](https://upsolve.org/); co-founders Rohan Pavuluri and Jonathan Petts, bankruptcy lawyers/judges, the Legal Services Corporation and access-to-justice community.
- **Public URL / discovery source:** [Upsolve impact](https://upsolve.org/impact/) and [founding/limitations](https://help.upsolve.org/hc/en-us/articles/360005366734-Is-Upsolve-real-Is-this-a-legitimate-service); discovered through legal-tech and access-to-justice collections.
- **What it does:** Screens users, guides them through financial questions, obtains documents, populates bankruptcy forms, and provides human support and review at no cost. Upsolve reports more than 26,000 people helped and over $1.1bn in debt erased.
- **Bureaucratic ritual:** Eligibility screening, exhaustive financial disclosure, document gathering, petition generation, court filing, trustee process, and discharge.
- **Technology's actual role:** A guided expert system turns legal forms into a step-by-step interview, with document/data automation and human quality safeguards.
- **Intended affect:** A “fresh start,” comprehensibility, and the ability to exercise a constitutional right without unaffordable legal fees.
- **Experienced affect:** **Participant-reported.** Upsolve publishes identified public reviewers describing the process as easy and fast, and one saying the outcome made them feel able to breathe again. These are real-user reviews curated by the maker. [Source](https://upsolve.org/impact/).
- **Civic problem / beneficiary / under-resourced relevance:** Bankruptcy relief usually requires up-front legal fees from people already unable to pay debts.
- **Power and contestability:** Users decide whether to file and can access the court, but the court/trustee remains authoritative. Upsolve screens out cases that need a lawyer and does not provide legal advice.
- **Durable artifact or capability:** A free national filing tool, educational library, review/support process, and legal advocacy around community justice advocates.
- **Classification / SB borrowing:** **Sparkle exemplar.** Borrow the emotional promise attached to a formal artifact: the petition is not paperwork, but a visible route to breathing room.
- **Concern/gap:** Reviews are self-selected; the tool cannot serve complex cases; bankruptcy remains consequential and public.
- **Evidence confidence / website readiness:** **High / ready.** Strong process, affect, under-resourced purpose, and durable tool.

### 8. JustFix Letter of Complaint

- **Dates/status:** Active current service in English and Spanish.
- **Geography/community:** New York City tenants across private, regulated, public and affordable housing.
- **Organisation / people / network:** [JustFix](https://www.justfix.org/) with tenant organisers, legal advocates, health providers, and housing-justice coalitions.
- **Public URL / discovery source:** [Letter of Complaint](https://app.justfix.org/en/loc/splash) and [partners](https://www.justfix.org/en/partners/); discovered via civic/legal-tech directories.
- **What it does:** Turns repair problems into a lawyer-reviewed letter, sends it free by USPS Certified Mail, creates proof that the landlord was notified, and texts the tenant to follow up.
- **Bureaucratic ritual:** Classify housing status and conditions, formally notify the landlord, retain proof, wait for response, and build an evidentiary trail for enforcement.
- **Technology's actual role:** Guided document assembly, housing-law logic, certified-mail fulfilment, status texts, and links to further legal help.
- **Intended affect:** Dignity, agency, safety, and the confidence to assert a right without facing a legal maze alone.
- **Experienced affect:** **Participant-reported.** A tenant says JustFix's letter achieved repairs when their own did not; another calls the accessible, easy-to-use platform a “game changer” and values the follow-up text. [Source](https://app.justfix.org/en/loc/splash).
- **Civic problem / beneficiary / under-resourced relevance:** Unsafe housing, power imbalance, retaliation fears and the cost of creating legally useful records.
- **Power and contestability:** It moves tenants from informal complaint to documented assertion and supports escalation, but landlords can still ignore the letter and the tool cannot enforce repairs.
- **Durable artifact or capability:** Certified notice, delivery record, follow-up history, reusable templates, and connections to tenant organisations.
- **Classification / SB borrowing:** **Sparkle exemplar.** Borrow the combination of a meaningful official artifact, an institutionally legible delivery channel, and a small human check-in after the ritual.
- **Concern/gap:** The pleasant tool does not remove retaliation risk or the burden of later enforcement. Its legal scope is local.
- **Evidence confidence / website readiness:** **High / ready.** Exceptionally clear website card: formal ritual, affect, artifact and increased agency.

### 9. Pathfinder

- **Dates/status:** Launched September 2024; active and expanding in 2026.
- **Geography/community:** US immigration attorneys and nonprofit legal-service providers serving people in immigration court.
- **Organisation / people / network:** [Mobile Pathways](https://www.mobilepathways.org/); founder Jeffrey O'Brien, immigration attorney Ruby Powers, Lawyers for Good Government and more than 100 partner organisations.
- **Public URL / discovery source:** [Pathfinder project account](https://www.mobilepathways.org/post/ai-powered-immigration-assistance-with-pathfinder) and [Mobile Pathways overview](https://www.mobilepathways.org/); discovered through the user's starter list.
- **What it does:** Uses a person's A-number to assemble case intelligence, monitor court changes and hearing dates, generate alerts, and support employment-authorisation workflows. The maker reports thousands of daily uses and a lower in-absentia removal rate among managed cases.
- **Bureaucratic ritual:** Case identification, docket monitoring, deadline/hearing notice, attorney preparation, appearance, and employment-authorisation follow-up.
- **Technology's actual role:** Gemini-powered search/analysis, machine learning, large-scale court-data processing, dashboards and real-time alerts.
- **Intended affect:** Replace chaos and uncertainty for practitioners with clarity, preparedness, direction and hope for clients.
- **Experienced affect:** **Participant-reported by practitioner, not service user.** Attorney Ruby Powers calls Pathfinder a game changer and says she cannot live without it; the tool makes judicial disparities visible. [Source](https://www.mobilepathways.org/). Immigrant affect is not evidenced.
- **Civic problem / beneficiary / under-resourced relevance:** Immigration representation is scarce; missed hearings can lead to removal, while nonprofit providers face volatile policy and huge caseloads.
- **Power and contestability:** Attorneys gain information and can act, but courts and immigration agencies remain authoritative. The system exposes judicial discrepancy but does not create an appeal right.
- **Durable artifact or capability:** Data infrastructure, alerts, dashboards, partner network, and repeatable practitioner capacity.
- **Classification / SB borrowing:** **Infrastructure awaiting public-facing Sparkle.** Borrow the idea that notification is not a minor feature but a protection against state harm.
- **Concern/gap:** Claims and metrics are maker-published; sensitive identifiers, model/data accuracy, consent and surveillance require scrutiny. The felt experience of immigrants is absent.
- **Evidence confidence / website readiness:** **Medium / near-miss or reserve.** Valuable work where Sparkle is unproven for the person subject to the bureaucracy.

### 10. Refugee.Info / Signpost

- **Dates/status:** First Refugee.Info deployment in 2015; active global network. AI-assisted response pilots have run in multiple countries since 2024.
- **Geography/community:** Refugees, migrants and crisis-affected communities across many countries; examples reviewed in Greece, Italy, Kenya, El Salvador and Colombia.
- **Organisation / people / network:** [International Rescue Committee](https://www.rescue.org/) and Signpost partners including Mercy Corps, local organisations and technology partners; associated with Signpost director André Heller.
- **Public URL / discovery source:** [IRC impact account](https://www.rescue.org/article/impact-signpost-bridging-information-gap-people-crisis), [Italy protection case](https://protection.interaction.org/case-examples/how-irc-italys-two-way-information-platform-contributes-to-protection-outcomes-exploring-eviction-of-refugees-in-italy/), and [AI risk reporting](https://apnews.com/article/9c5fae949c429c38019feabaeec5fafa); discovered through humanitarian innovation sources.
- **What it does:** Runs multilingual websites, service maps, social channels and human-moderated helpdesks that answer questions about documents, rights, housing, health, safety and integration. User questions also reveal protection trends such as forced eviction.
- **Bureaucratic ritual:** Discover a right/service, ask a contextual question, receive verified navigation, find a provider, and escalate complex cases to a person.
- **Technology's actual role:** Omnichannel content and case management, service mapping and messaging; newer pilots use multiple LLMs to draft or retrieve answers while local teams vet information and escalate sensitive questions.
- **Intended affect:** Trust, control, self-determination, safety, and being met in one's own language and channel.
- **Experienced affect:** **Observed.** InterAction reports that Refugee.Info Italy built trust with a group-chat user base of nearly 25,000. **Participant story:** IRC describes a woman finding urgently needed information in a language she understood. These are useful but not a systematic affect evaluation.
- **Civic problem / beneficiary / under-resourced relevance:** People facing displacement must navigate fast-changing, fragmented services with limited reliable information and severe resource constraints.
- **Power and contestability:** Two-way questions help communities set the information agenda and can expose protection risks. The platform does not decide immigration claims, and it can refer/escalate; meaningful governance over data and AI remains essential.
- **Durable artifact or capability:** Local editorial/moderation teams, multilingual content, service maps, helpdesk infrastructure and a network deployable in a new crisis.
- **Classification / SB borrowing:** **Sparkle exemplar with risk caveat.** Borrow the reciprocal loop: the helpdesk not only explains bureaucracy but learns from people where the bureaucracy is failing.
- **Concern/gap:** The AP identifies error, privacy, hostile access and power-imbalance risks in AI-enabled humanitarian systems; connectivity and local governance can be weak.
- **Evidence confidence / website readiness:** **High on service model, medium on affect / ready with caveat.** Strong example if the risk and human escalation are part of the card.

### 11. Community Health Toolkit

- **Dates/status:** Active open-source digital public good with deployments and a current product community.
- **Geography/community:** Community health workers, supervisors and health ministries in hard-to-reach and low-resource settings, especially across Africa and Asia.
- **Organisation / people / network:** [Medic](https://medic.org/) as technical steward; Community Health Toolkit contributors, ministries, Living Goods, Muso and frontline health workers.
- **Public URL / discovery source:** [Community Health Toolkit](https://communityhealthtoolkit.org/), [UHC mode](https://docs.communityhealthtoolkit.org/building/uhc-mode/uhc-mode-overview/), and [Medic UX research](https://medic.org/stories/continuous-discovery-how-we-create-a-better-cht-ui-ux/); discovered through the Digital Public Goods Registry and UNDP Digital X.
- **What it does:** Provides open components for household registration, longitudinal records, task schedules, messaging, decision support and performance views so community health workers can deliver care offline and at the last mile.
- **Bureaucratic ritual:** Household enrolment, visit planning, assessment, referral, follow-up, supervision, reporting and health-system handoff.
- **Technology's actual role:** Offline-first Android/web/feature-phone tools, configurable workflows, analytics and interoperability; no AI is essential to the core case.
- **Intended affect:** Tools should be intuitive and pleasurable for health workers, reduce uncertainty, and help trusted local people reach every family.
- **Experienced affect:** Medic documents interviews, effort surveys, shadowing and usability tests with workers, but the reviewed public summaries mostly report needs and usability problems, not emotions. **Affect not evidenced.**
- **Civic problem / beneficiary / under-resourced relevance:** Health systems with too few professionals rely on community workers operating with low connectivity, high workload and fragmented records.
- **Power and contestability:** Workers gain decision support and visibility; ministries and programme implementers define workflows and performance measures. Patient appeal and worker control over monitoring are not visible at platform level.
- **Durable artifact or capability:** Open core, reference apps, design guidance, documentation, forum and implementation community.
- **Classification / SB borrowing:** **Infrastructure awaiting Sparkle.** Borrow co-design with frontline workers, offline resilience, and care continuity as an organisational ritual.
- **Concern/gap:** Digitisation can also increase surveillance and task burden. The relationship between performance dashboards, worker autonomy and patient consent needs project-specific evidence.
- **Evidence confidence / website readiness:** **High on capability, low on affect / infrastructure section.** Strong “valuable work, sparkle unproven” candidate.

### 12. Haqdarshak

- **Dates/status:** Founded in 2016; active and expanding AI-enabled welfare and finance services.
- **Geography/community:** Underserved households, informal workers, rural communities, micro-businesses and women agents across India.
- **Organisation / people / network:** [Haqdarshak Empowerment Solutions](https://www.haqdarshak.com/); founder Aniket Doegar and a network of more than 50,000 trained local agents, most of them women.
- **Public URL / discovery source:** [Haqdarshak platform and impact](https://www.haqdarshak.com/), [NITI Frontier Tech profile](https://frontiertech.niti.gov.in/story/a-revolution-for-indias-welfare-how-ai-is-helping-families-access-public-schemes/), and [Acumen case study](https://acumen.org/case-studies/haqdarshak/); discovered through HiiL and public-interest-tech mappings.
- **What it does:** Maps thousands of public and private schemes, identifies likely eligibility, and combines multilingual digital discovery with door-to-door application help from trained community agents. It reports reaching 7.6 million families and unlocking $2.2bn in benefits.
- **Bureaucratic ritual:** Discover an entitlement, screen eligibility, obtain identity/supporting records, complete applications, visit offices where necessary, and track fulfilment.
- **Technology's actual role:** AI-driven multilingual and multimodal discovery, a schemes database and agent app; the decisive interface is often a trusted human intermediary.
- **Intended affect:** Demystification, trust, local support, dignity, and economic/leadership agency for women agents.
- **Experienced affect:** **Participant-reported.** In a 255-citizen COVID-relief survey, 70% reported being highly satisfied with Haqdarshak's support. [Maker-hosted source](https://yojanacard.haqdarshak.com/portfolio/07-covid-relief-at-the-last-mile/). **Observed:** Acumen reports agents gaining income, autonomy and community-leader roles.
- **Civic problem / beneficiary / under-resourced relevance:** Welfare exists but information, language, literacy, identity documents and application support are missing at the last mile.
- **Power and contestability:** Agents help people navigate and challenge document gaps, but agencies retain eligibility decisions. The model gives local women a role and livelihood, while also introducing a paid/intermediated layer.
- **Durable artifact or capability:** Schemes knowledge base, multilingual platform, trained agent network, application support and the Yojana Card service channel.
- **Classification / SB borrowing:** **Sparkle exemplar.** Borrow “assisted tech” and treat the trusted local role—not the AI model—as the key civic technology.
- **Concern/gap:** Some services involve nominal fees; reliance on agents may reproduce intermediary power. Impact figures and satisfaction evidence are partly self-reported.
- **Evidence confidence / website readiness:** **High / ready.** Adds geography, AI, human relationships, livelihoods and direct under-resourced capacity.

### 13. OpenCRVS

- **Dates/status:** Conceived from 2015 onward; active digital public good with country proofs, pilots and implementations.
- **Geography/community:** Civil-registration authorities, local/health registration agents, and families in low-resource settings; documented cases include Bangladesh, Zambia, Nigeria, Cameroon, Mali and Niue.
- **Organisation / people / network:** [OpenCRVS](https://www.opencrvs.org/), originating in Plan International's digital birth-registration work; co-founders Annina Wersun and Ed Duffus, design lead Jonathan Pye-Finch, governments and implementation partners.
- **Public URL / discovery source:** [OpenCRVS overview](https://www.opencrvs.org/), [Bangladesh case](https://www.opencrvs.org/about-us/case-studies/bangladesh), and [case-study index](https://www.opencrvs.org/about-us/case-studies); discovered through the DPG Registry and UNDP Digital X.
- **What it does:** Replaces fragmented paper birth/death registration with configurable workflows that work offline, support community and health-facility notification, detect duplicates, update families by SMS and issue certificates. In a Bangladesh pilot, birth completeness increased from 14% to 63% and death completeness from 2% to 47% within a year of the event.
- **Bureaucratic ritual:** Notify a life event, declare it with evidence, validate, approve/register, print/issue a certificate, correct records and connect identity to other services.
- **Technology's actual role:** Open microservices, offline work queues, mobile/desktop interfaces, interoperability, deduplication, verifiable credentials, SMS and auditable role-based access.
- **Intended affect:** Recognition, certainty, inclusion, and confidence that a child's or relative's existence has been recorded.
- **Experienced affect:** **Participant-reported via maker case study.** Families in the Bangladesh pilot appreciated fewer visits, the streamlined process and SMS status updates. This is experience evidence, though not a direct emotional quotation.
- **Civic problem / beneficiary / under-resourced relevance:** Unregistered births can block education, health and social protection; families may otherwise make long trips and unofficial payments.
- **Power and contestability:** Formal correction/approval workflows and audit trails offer some traceability, while state registrars retain authority. Identity infrastructure creates profound privacy and exclusion risks if rules or records are wrong.
- **Durable artifact or capability:** Open platform, configuration/implementation method, country community, documentation and interoperable civil-registration infrastructure.
- **Classification / SB borrowing:** **Sparkle exemplar.** Borrow the idea that status updates and bringing declaration into trusted community/health rituals can turn “being counted” into a felt civic promise.
- **Concern/gap:** Official pilot reporting needs independent corroboration. Security, correction access, data sharing and connection to coercive identity systems require local scrutiny.
- **Evidence confidence / website readiness:** **High / ready.** A strong global public-service case if the website treats recognition and surveillance as a live tension.

- **Classification / SB borrowing:** **Sparkle exemplar / institutional ancestor.** Borrow the hybrid public counter: technology plus a recognisable local person, place and rite of assistance.

### 17. FixMyStreet

- **Dates/status:** Launched in the UK in 2007; active open-source platform with current deployments in several countries and local authorities.

## What these lanes suggest about Sparkle

- **Sparkle may be the removal of a ritual, not its decoration.** Clear My Record is powerful because an eligible person no longer has to petition at all.
- **Status is affective infrastructure.** OpenCRVS SMS updates, Pathfinder alerts and JustFix follow-up reduce the dread of not knowing whether an institution has seen you.
- **The best technology sometimes sits behind a person.** Haqdarshak agents, Digital Centre entrepreneurs, community health workers and Signpost moderators make the system relational and locally intelligible.
- **A durable artifact changes posture.** A certified repair letter, bankruptcy petition, birth certificate, cleared record or submitted benefits application lets a person approach power differently.
- **Frontline-worker affect matters, but cannot substitute for service-user affect.** Re:form shows the two can reinforce each other; Pathfinder currently evidences mainly the practitioner side.
- **Digital sovereignty is not yet Sparkle.** Open code, modularity and local ownership are valuable civic preconditions, but they do not show how the encounter felt or whether a claimant could challenge it.
- **The key warning is faster exclusion.** Registries, case-management tools and AI can make a bad rule more efficient. A public card should always identify correction, appeal, human escalation and who retains decision power.
