# Sparkle Calibration

Status: working hypothesis for the longlist, not a settled definition.

The six starter cases are deliberately heterogeneous. They should reveal where the written rubric conflicts with the project's current gut feeling, rather than act as six examples that the rubric must automatically validate.

## Scale

- **0 — absent or contrary:** the dimension is not present, or the project works against it.
- **1 — adjacent or claimed:** the dimension is contextual, weak, or asserted without evidence.
- **2 — designed and observable:** the project meaningfully addresses the dimension, with some supporting evidence.
- **3 — central and evidenced:** the dimension is essential to the work and supported by strong primary or independent evidence.

Scores are discussion prompts, not a total Sparkle score. A lopsided profile may be more informative than a high aggregate.

The affect score concerns affect designed into the form or encounter. Participant research can strengthen or challenge that reading, but it is not required for a project to score strongly.

## Case notes

### Sparkle Border Authority

- **Process:** preserves declarations, screening, approval, rejection, visas, checkpoints, admin overrides, and a final handoff.
- **Technology:** a complete digital service and printing workflow, but not emerging technology in the AI sense.
- **Affect:** play, co-created fiction, anticipation, and belonging were designed into the encounter. The diary says people played along fully, but direct participant evidence has not yet been preserved in the research record.
- **Civic boost:** demonstrates a transferable design move, but the original party implementation did not solve a civic service problem.
- **Power:** the gate remained real. The experiment makes enforcement theatrical, but it does not necessarily make it more contestable.
- **Calibration tension:** if this is the origin case, neither AI nor direct civic impact can currently be absolute requirements for something to feel Sparkly.
- **Sources:** [prototype](https://sparkle-border-authority.vercel.app/); [repository account](../README.md#what-ive-built-so-far).

### Papers, Please

- **Process:** document inspection, identity verification, rule changes, classification, approval, rejection, detention, quotas, and wages.
- **Technology:** digital simulation is integral to putting the audience in the inspector's role, but it is not an AI or contemporary emerging-technology project.
- **Affect:** discomfort, complicity, pressure, empathy, and the seduction of efficient rule-following are generated through the mechanics rather than added as decoration. This is independently discussed in games scholarship.
- **Civic boost:** produces civic understanding and moral reflection, but does not create a direct service capability.
- **Power:** exceptionally strong at revealing how distant rules become intimate administrative harm.
- **Calibration tension:** establishes that Sparkle may involve unsettling affect rather than optimism in the ordinary sense.
- **Sources:** [official press kit](https://papersplea.se/presskit/); [Game Studies analysis](https://gamestudies.org/1701/articles/morrissette); [Press Start review](https://press-start.gla.ac.uk/press-start/article/view/70).

### Cart Life

- **Process:** permits, payments, custody, court dates, timekeeping, retail administration, and the paperwork surrounding precarious work.
- **Technology:** the simulation format makes routine pressure playable, but the work is not AI-native.
- **Affect:** boredom, fatigue, frustration, isolation, precarity, and humour arise from repetitive tasks and constrained time. Independent reviews report these effects directly.
- **Civic boost:** builds understanding of precarious work but does not deliver an institutional intervention.
- **Power:** makes the interaction between administrative obligations and economic precarity palpable.
- **Calibration tension:** it is less a redesign of bureaucracy than an affective encounter with bureaucracy. Does representation count, or must Sparkle intervene in a real process?
- **Sources:** [MIT Docubase](https://docubase.mit.edu/project/cart-life/); [Destructoid review](https://www.destructoid.com/reviews/review-cart-life/).

### Document Assembly Line

- **Process:** converts legal questions into guided interviews, assembled court forms, and increasingly simplified e-filing.
- **Technology:** open-source document automation is central; newer LITEfile work uses AI for document identification, filing-code suggestions, pre-filling, and issue spotting.
- **Affect:** accessibility and reduced complexity are explicit design intentions. The current source set does not establish how self-represented litigants actually felt.
- **Civic boost:** gives courts, legal-aid organisations, and volunteers reusable infrastructure for helping people navigate legal processes.
- **Power:** increases procedural agency without changing the authority or underlying rules of the court.
- **Calibration tension:** may be exemplary civic infrastructure while remaining emotionally neutral. Is reduced anxiety itself Sparkle if it is evidenced?
- **Sources:** [Suffolk LIT Lab](https://suffolklitlab.org/); [Document Assembly Line](https://assemblyline.suffolklitlab.org/docs/); [2025 update including LITEfile](https://suffolklitlab.org/document-assembly-line-q4-2025-update/).

### Talk to the City

- **Process:** ingests public input, extracts arguments, clusters related views, and produces navigable reports for deliberation and decision support.
- **Technology:** LLM analysis is essential rather than decorative.
- **Affect:** the stated ambitions include helping people feel heard and understand the discourse around them. The reviewed sources mostly describe maker intent; independent participant-reported affect remains thin.
- **Civic boost:** can make large volumes of qualitative participation more legible, although the connection from report to binding decision varies by deployment.
- **Power:** makes viewpoints visible but gives the summarisation pipeline considerable representational authority.
- **Calibration tension:** technical and civic fit is strong, while the felt encounter is less clear. Does Sparkle require an experience participants themselves enter?
- **Sources:** [project overview](https://ai.objectives.institute/talk-to-the-city-1); [introduction](https://ai.objectives.institute/blog/introducing-talk-to-the-city-our-collective-deliberation-tool); [GitHub](https://github.com/AIObjectives/talk-to-the-city-reports).

### Project Mirror

- **Process:** evaluator selection, value constitutions, scoring, ranking, aggregation, deliberation, and an inspectable decision record.
- **Technology:** synthetic evaluator agents and multi-agent deliberation are central.
- **Affect:** the showcase produced fascination and discomfort around having an evaluative stance inferred. The diary records this, but the participant reflection should be attached as primary evidence before a public affect claim is made.
- **Civic boost:** expands participation experimentally inside a cohort, but has not yet demonstrated a boost for an under-resourced civic context.
- **Power:** exposes values and aggregation rules, while creating unresolved consent and authority problems around inferred representation.
- **Calibration tension:** a process may be transparent and affectively charged while still granting AI an uncomfortable form of authority.
- **Sources:** [Political Technology Awards](https://2025.newspeak.house/awards); [repository account](../README.md#what-ive-built-so-far).

## Provisional definition

> **Sparkle is an affective intervention in a consequential procedure: it lets people feel and understand how rules and power are moving through an encounter, creates more room to participate, question, or act, and leaves behind a capability or ritual rather than a decorative veneer.**

This definition currently treats emerging technology as Sparkle Bureaucracy's chosen arena, not as the universal essence of Sparkle. That distinction is unresolved.

## Questions the longlist must pressure-test

1. Is emerging technology part of the definition of **Sparkle**, or a programme constraint for **Sparkle Bureaucracy**?
2. Must Sparkle improve how a process feels, or can it deliberately produce dread, guilt, grief, or moral discomfort?
3. Does representing a bureaucracy count, as in *Papers, Please* and *Cart Life*, or must the project intervene in a real procedure?
4. Does “optimistic” describe the emotion, the possibility of change, or the project's underlying politics?
5. Is civic imagination a sufficient civic boost, or must users gain a concrete capability?
6. Can a project be Sparkly when the underlying rule and allocation of power remain unchanged?
7. Is legibility enough, or must meaningful appeal or contestability be added?
8. Does reduced anxiety, confusion, or humiliation count as affective design even when a project avoids theatricality?
9. Whose affect matters: the service user, the bureaucrat, the audience, or all three?
10. When the website makes a claim about actual audience response, what is the minimum acceptable evidence—and how should that remain separate from our reading of the designed affect?
11. Must a durable artifact be adopted by an institution, or is a reusable cultural pattern enough?
12. Is a one-off event Sparkly if it changes relationships but leaves no formal tool behind?
13. Can playful design become anti-Sparkle by making coercion friendlier without making it weaker or more contestable?
14. Is the priority under-resourced contexts an inclusion rule, a scoring boost, or a commitment governing Sparkle Bureaucracy's own work?
15. Does every exemplar need all dimensions, or is Sparkle best understood as a family resemblance across projects?

## Working inferences to revisit after discovery

- Affect is broader than delight; the most useful ancestors often create productive discomfort.
- Aesthetic whimsy is neither necessary nor sufficient.
- Technology should change the encounter or capability, not merely decorate it.
- Direct civic utility distinguishes an exemplar from an ancestor, but ancestors remain essential to explaining the idea.
- Power should remain visible. A friendly interface that hides coercion is a warning case.
- Designed affect can be established through the work's form, mechanics and presentation. Claims about actual audience response remain separate and should not be made without reception evidence.
