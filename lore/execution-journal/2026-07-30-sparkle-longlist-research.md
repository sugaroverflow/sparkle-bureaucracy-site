## 2026-07-30T00:00:00Z - Sparkle Inspiration Longlist Research

### Goal

Build an evidence-aware research engine for explaining Sparkle Bureaucracy through projects, organisations, source directories, people, and networks, while keeping projects as the primary unit.

### Changes

- Added `research-longlist/` with a reusable research method, evidence model, and website-card format.
- Calibrated six known cases against emerging technology, bureaucracy, affect, civic capacity, power, and durable capability.
- Created linked, deduplicated longlists of 25 projects, 25 organisations, 25 directories or archives, and 25 people or networks.
- Retained the wider discovery pool and full evidence profiles in three lane-based research files.
- Proposed 10 website inspirations, 6 valuable projects where Sparkle is absent or unproven, and a 10-project private reserve including a founder-nominated priority challenger.
- Added the research directory to the repository README.
- Added a separate movements-and-tactics research layer, seeded with Mexico's Glitter Revolution and Portland's Operation Inflation, so collective affective practices are not misrepresented as authored projects.

### Decisions

- Treat the raw 40–50-project pool as discovery material and the 25-project set as the evidence-enriched longlist.
- Treat emerging technology as Sparkle Bureaucracy's current arena, not yet as an essential property of Sparkle itself.
- Define affect broadly enough to include relief, dignity, discomfort, certainty, empathy, and productive disagreement, not only delight.
- Treat designed affect and experienced response as separate evidence questions. A work's form can establish its affective ambition without an event, survey, publication, or participant quotation; reception evidence is required only for claims about what an audience actually felt.
- Classify projects by their relationship to the research question: exemplar, ancestor, infrastructure awaiting Sparkle, or warning case.
- Make power, contestability, and institutional follow-through visible in the editorial judgment rather than rewarding friendly interfaces by default.
- Treat affective presentation as part of a project's operative design when it is the mechanism that makes otherwise inaccessible systems and technical power perceptible. Following founder review, `Models All the Way Down` entered the inspired set and `Cart Life` moved to the reserve.
- Do not turn the inclusion rubric into a pass/fail definition of inspiration. `Models All the Way Down` has an indirect bureaucratic relationship—the dataset is an administrative artifact rather than a bureaucracy—but remains valuable because it demonstrates affective legibility exceptionally well.
- Keep movements beside the project portfolio rather than forcing them into it. Research the political demand, institutional power, affective tactic, replication, risk and attribution separately.

### Tradeoffs

- The sprint prioritised breadth and editorial usefulness over exhaustive literature review.
- Evidence depth is uneven: the selected website set is supported well enough for drafting, but every public card should still receive a final source audit.
- Some strong civic projects remain in the reserve because the first public set would otherwise overrepresent legal access or digital deliberation.
- Interviews and original participant research were intentionally deferred for the later events phase.

### Risks

- The proposed definition remains a working hypothesis until the founder resolves whether technology, hopefulness, power transfer, and under-resourced civic use are requirements or programme commitments.
- Several affect claims rely on project-hosted case studies rather than independent evaluation.
- Warning cases can be misread as endorsements unless the website's classification and unresolved tensions are visually explicit.
- Dynamic project pages, programme status, and organisational ownership may change and need checking immediately before publication.

### Verification

- Three research subagents independently covered affect and organisational ritual, public-service and under-resourced civic capacity, and AI and participatory governance.
- The primary agent reviewed and synthesised all three discovery files.
- Verified that each final longlist contains exactly 25 entries.
- Checked the repository diff for whitespace errors and reviewed the local cross-links among method, calibration, longlists, evidence profiles, and selection.
- No interviews, participant outreach, or empirical testing were conducted.

### Demo Impact

Sparkle Bureaucracy can now explain itself through a small, varied public portfolio rather than an abstract rubric. The larger linked corpus remains available as a private research engine for future newsletters, events, and substitutions.

### Customer-Facing Context

The research makes the project's central trust claim more concrete: an affective public-service encounter should reveal rather than disguise power, create meaningful room to act or contest, and leave behind civic capability. It also records where evidence is missing instead of converting aspiration into a user-experience claim.

## 2026-07-30T12:10:18Z - Movements and Affective Tactics Deep Research

### Goal

Use Mexico's Glitter Revolution and Portland's inflatable anti-ICE protests as anchors for a deeper, globally varied investigation of movements whose material, ritual, costume, choreography or humour offers a useful account of Sparkle.

### Changes

- Added `research-longlist/movements-deep-research.md` with a calibrated movement-specific rubric and six discovery lanes.
- Built a sourced longlist of exactly 25 movements and affective tactics across multiple geographies and institutional targets.
- Completed twelve deeper evidence profiles that separate designed affect, experienced response, strategic effect and political outcome.
- Recommended eight cases for a first public “Movements we're inspired by” section and retained thematic pairings as a private reserve.
- Expanded the website selection from two movement seeds to eight concise, evidence-linked cards.
- Recorded seven findings about Sparkle and eight design principles for Sparkle Bureaucracy's own work.

### Decisions

- Research the **movement** and the **tactic** as linked but distinct units: the former carries demand, people and political context; the latter carries the affective mechanism and reusable repertoire.
- Define movement durability broadly enough to include a score, garment, cadence, gesture, collective role or sound protocol.
- Require an institutional target and an inhabitable participant action; visual memorability by itself is insufficient.
- Treat virality as evidence of circulation, not civic impact.
- Keep both founder anchors in the website set because they do different work: glitter marks authority, while inflatables reorganise participant roles and the evidentiary record.
- Use Holograms for Freedom as a productive near-miss: its technological concept clearly explains a coercive law, but evidence for durable civic capability is much weaker than evidence for spectacle.
- Treat culturally grounded forms such as haka as sources of principles, not reproducible formats.

### Tradeoffs

- Breadth was prioritised across the full 25; twelve cases received deep profiles and the remaining thirteen received source-backed longlist records.
- Policy outcomes are not attributed to individual symbols or performances where a larger coalition and political process produced the change.
- Current movement research relies on public reporting and published accounts; no activists were contacted, consistent with the decision to reserve interviews for Sparkle Bureaucracy's own events.

### Risks

- Short website cards can aestheticise danger or strip a tactic from its demand, geography and movement authorship.
- Collective tactics rarely have clean singular authorship; attribution must be checked again before publication.
- Repressive political contexts and legal status can change, so current cases need a fresh safety and source check at publication time.
- The movement selection currently draws heavily from feminist, queer and democracy struggles because their affective repertoires are unusually well documented; future newsletters should keep widening disability, labour, Indigenous, migration and mutual-aid coverage.

### Verification

- Checked that the movement/tactic table contains exactly 25 numbered records.
- Used movement, archive, institutional, scholarly and reputable reporting sources; lead databases were not treated as final evidence where stronger sources were available.
- Reviewed each website candidate for a distinct portfolio role rather than selecting multiple variants of the same visual tactic.
- Rechecked that designed affect remains independent of formal audience-evaluation requirements.

### Demo Impact

The website can now show that Sparkle is not limited to friendly public services or polished projects. It can be oppositional, grief-bearing, ridiculous or accusatory while still building civic agency, procedural understanding and reusable capacity.

### Next Recommended Step

Choose whether the public section launches with all eight movement cards or four paired cards, then source-audit image rights and write final 80–120 word versions in the site's voice.
