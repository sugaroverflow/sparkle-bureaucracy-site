# Sparkle Bureaucracy - Faculty Feedback Brief

This is the Google-Doc-friendly feedback surface for faculty and mentors.

The prototype is **Sparkle Bureaucracy**: an experiment lab for prototyping optimistic organisational futures for the age of AI. The open question is not "what is the prototype?" The open question is which first experiments the lab should run, what evidence those experiments need, and how to make the lab credible against the fellowship criteria.

---

## 1. What I Am Building

Sparkle Bureaucracy is an experiment lab for testing whether bureaucratic rituals can be redesigned to be more legible, participatory, and humane without pretending the underlying power dynamics disappear.

It starts from a pattern I have already built and observed:

> intake -> process -> decision -> artifact -> handoff

That pattern appears in borders, evaluations, voting, public consultation, queues, permissions, digital identity, and AI-mediated governance. The lab runs small experiments in these systems, publishes what happened, and turns the results into reusable patterns, artifacts, and evidence.

---

## 2. Why This Is Political Technology

The project sits at the intersection of:

- Civic technology and public-sector AI.
- Participatory democracy and collective judgment.
- Bureaucratic experience design.
- Synthetic voice, identity, and legitimacy in AI-mediated systems.
- Research infrastructure for public learning.

The goal is not to produce policy advice. The goal is to run concrete experiments that make institutional procedures more inspectable, discussable, and redesignable.

---

## 3. What Already Exists

- **Sparkle Border Authority:** a full border-crossing ritual for a live event, including forms, screening, visa printing, checkpoints, admin overrides, and live stats.
- **Project Mirror:** a multi-agent evaluator that inferred value constitutions from cohort members' public records, then ran project rankings and social-choice aggregation.
- **PoliTech Awards process:** public, versioned evaluation of political technology projects.
- **Field notes and research inventory:** ongoing notes on legitimacy, permission, synthetic representation, public-sector AI, and civic infrastructure.

## 5. Candidate First Experiments

### A. Agent-Assisted Prototype Diary

Role: lab research infrastructure.

Question: Can an agent help turn scattered field notes, links, conversations, and feedback into structured weekly diary drafts, experiment cards, prior-art candidates, and collaborator suggestions?

Risk: may be seen as too meta unless connected to public learning and community validation.

### B. Faculty Feedback Loop

Role: lab governance / critique process.

Question: Does structured feedback produce more actionable faculty critique than asking people to review the whole project at once?

Risk: may be too small unless paired with a public/civic-facing experiment.

### C. Collaborator and Prior-Art Scout

Role: lab network development.

Question: Can an agent help identify relevant collaborators and prior art from the lab's field notes without producing shallow or extractive outreach?

Risk: needs strong human review and evidence-backed suggestions.

### D. Synthetic Voice in Participatory Channels

Role: civic-facing experiment.

Question: How should representatives and civic organisations handle constituent feedback when synthetic voice is cheap to generate?

Risk: hardening channels against synthetic feedback could also make participation harder for real constituents.

### E. Hybrid Liquid Democracy Election

Role: civic-facing experiment.

Question: What happens when a bounded community runs an election using both paper and digital channels, with visible delegation and revocation?

Risk: voting experiments create legitimacy expectations even when framed as exploratory.

### F. DMV / Queue and Classification Ritual

Role: civic/public-service-facing experiment.

Question: Can a frustrating service encounter be made more legible and humane without changing the underlying policy?

Risk: making a bad process feel better may obscure the need to change the underlying policy.

### G. Digital Identity / Verification Ritual

Role: civic/public-service-facing experiment.

Question: What does it mean to prove who you are when identity verification, synthetic media, and AI agents collide?

Risk: identity systems can easily become exclusionary.

## 7. My Current Belief

The strongest near-term structure is:

1. Treat Sparkle Bureaucracy as the assessed experiment lab prototype.
2. Use the agent-assisted diary and faculty feedback loop as the lab's operating infrastructure.
3. Choose one public/civic-facing experiment as the first flagship lab test.
4. Turn every candidate experiment into a comparable card with hypothesis, method, evidence, risks, and date.

What would change my mind:

- Faculty say the experiment lab is too broad unless one public/civic-facing experiment is clearly primary.
- A bounded community offers a concrete date for a liquid democracy or synthetic voice test.
- Prior art shows that one candidate is obsolete or predictably doomed.
- A mentor identifies a sharper civic community and user need for one option.
