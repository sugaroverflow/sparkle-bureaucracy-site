## The speech

As AI continues to improve and permeate through everyday lives, I find myself watching the gap between the people that have AI access and those that don't increase - and that worries me. Growing up in the US as the child of immigrants, I've seen what happens to skilled workers from other countries. I've seen the neuroscientists and engineers from abroad work at gas stations or drive taxis to get their children educated in America and get them better opportunities for success - all because their degrees weren't legible to the system.

I believe AI is going to make immigrants out of us all. I believe it's going to widen this gap, make it more and more expensive to access, to learn, and to teach. Sparkle Bureaucracy, as you heard earlier this year, was originally about a network of people using AI to explore optimistic organisational and service futures - because SB is my life. It's working in and outside of different government institutions, it's volunteering at hack nights across the US from Boston, to New York City, to Toronto, Ottawa, and London (the one in Ontario), to understanding that access to good civic services digitally is so crucial in democracy. So that's where the name comes from - running experiments that illustrate the power of technology and how these emerging innovations can help us envision the possibilities in govtech.

But SB evolved over the course of this fellowship. I came into thinking that I wanted to run experiments on things like DMVs - what does a better car licence renewal process look like? what does liquid democracy look like if the election results are being livestreamed while you can still change your vote? Running an immigration scheme at my birthday and giving people different types of passports and visa types and credentials - what sort of conversations did that spark?

And the experimentation went beyond bureaucratic processes - I ran the Cohort Reunion as a spy game - in order to circumvent the fact that nobody in my cohort really wanted to answer the question of "how is your prototype going?" so would there be a way I could get these two groups of people to interact with each other in a way that was "Sparkly" and fun and engaging so they could get to know each other's expertise, projects, and socialize? It was a resounding success because competition and games help lower the bar to social interactions.

I started hosting clawclubs in response to the sudden influx of the technology in the beginning of this year - and because I had been using and testing it in ways that were exciting and I wanted a community with whom I could share ideas, learn what they were building, and be excited. It was also a success - at times lightly attended 10-12 people, other times we had up to 60 people in the hall, all building and sharing, or even selling (lol) their hardware for open claw.

But I found myself reflecting on the prototype. While I love both these communities - the one that cares about tech for good, the one that cares about emerging technologies and building like open claw, and many of the communities I met in the Hall throughout this fellowship - the RightsCon folks, the Protocol for Publishers, the vTaiwan delegation, the Technologist Journalists, etc (we can pull some more from the events list at newspeak.house), I found that they helped me identify, collect, and narrow the characteristics of the community that I wanted to build.

In parallel, the fellowship and the prototype were also about building confidence. I had to grapple with the questions of why me? (in that why am I the person to do this?) and why now? (as why is this the time that yet another community/network must be built) and why not the others (as in there are so many great networks in London doing things around AI so what makes mine so special or me so unique that I must start a new thing). I have yet been able to answer this question to a degree of confidence, but I've learned that perhaps for now - since Hannah O'Rourke says that legitimacy is constructed - I must borrow the answers of those around me who have reflected to me that the overlap of sparkle x bureaucracy is unique, that I _am_ and seem to be an expert in AI when I speak about agents at events.

The thing that I can say with some confidence is that now is the window of opportunity to close the gap. As models become sufficiently more advanced, and we move our agents to the cloud, operate them remotely, and compute starts to become cheaper (in the long run), this gap will inevitably widen and most of the population will use AI tools that they don't even understand. I would like them to understand what they're using and I would like certain communities, those aligned with civic tech, tech for good, open source, gov tech, to be prepared for what's coming and how they can use these tools to continue to build new possibilities for themselves.

It's why I was so excited to be part of the AI & Campaigning conference. While campaigners weren't "my people" per se, working with Hannah on the contextualization of their problems and building them Campaign Factory - helped me realize how important it is to show people the power of these tools. Someone I met told me that Campaign Factory built for her what it would take her 5 months to research.

Similarly, my keynote at the Charity Evaluators Festival 2026 on Evaluation 2030 focused on how to shift your thinking from being prompt based to systems based in lieu of agents coming soon to you. Before evaluators are presented with vendors asking them to purchase things, I'd like them to understand how to articulate their work into a system and then plug agents into that system. There were like 15 people lined up to tell me their specific stories, how they work, and ask advice for where they could implement agents.

In the moment, these engagements perhaps didn't fit into a single mandate, but reflecting back on this past year, I think they were all pieces of this puzzle (and there's more of them in the pivotal moments that we can fit into this story and/or into the writeup).

In building all these projects, engaging with all these people, and considering these implications of this technology (through things like our AI safety study group where we read and discussed various materials on AI safety and invited people from neighboring institutions to join us), I believe I have a clearer vision for what SB is.

However, I don't know if "sparkle" "bureaucracy" is the right name. It used to be about making bureaucracy more sparkly, and I do believe that's my long-term life's mission, but as long as this window of gap is open for AI, I want to be here. I want to be someone who understands the technology sufficiently enough, who tests the latest technology, and who can build the bridge from the tech to the people that I'm speaking to in order to translate what that technology does, what it means for their work, and how they could utilize it to amplify themselves.

But I don't yet know what the new name of this network will be, so for this prototype, I present to you the accumulation of experiments, projects, and ideas that led me here and the roadmap towards what's next.
