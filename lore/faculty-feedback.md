# Faculty Feedback Tracker

This is the repo-side tracker for faculty critique. The commentable faculty surface should be a Google Doc generated from [faculty-feedback-brief.md](./faculty-feedback-brief.md); this file records what was asked, what came back, and how the project changed.

Use this file before and after faculty conversations:

1. State the decision.
2. Name the options.
3. Say what I currently believe.
4. Say what would change my mind.
5. Record the feedback.
6. Record what changed.

---

## Current Decision Needing Feedback

### Decision

Sparkle Bureaucracy is the assessed prototype: an experiment lab. Which first experiments should the lab run, in what order, and what evidence would make the lab credible against the fellowship criteria?

### Options

| Option | Role in the experiment lab | Why it might be strong | Main concern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Agent-assisted prototype diary | Lab research infrastructure | Directly supports diary requirements and shows AI-native knowledge production | May be seen as meta unless connected to public learning and community validation |
| Faculty feedback loop | Lab governance / critique process | Produces clear evidence of expert feedback changing the lab's experiment sequence | Needs a Google Doc surface and a disciplined response log |
| Collaborator and prior-art scout | Lab network development | Fits the Sparkle Bureaucracy network thesis | Needs careful evidence and avoids superficial outreach |
| Synthetic voice in participatory channels | Civic-facing experiment | Strong political technology fit around AI-era public participation | More complex, higher ethics and scope risk |
| Hybrid liquid democracy election | Civic-facing experiment | Concrete community test with real engagement evidence | Requires a willing community, dates, and legitimacy guardrails |
| DMV / queue ritual | Civic/public-service-facing experiment | Easy to understand and explain | Needs access to real users or practitioners to avoid pure theatre |
| Digital identity / verification ritual | Civic/public-service-facing experiment | Strong AI governance connection | High sensitivity and exclusion risk |

### Current Belief

The strongest near-term path is:

1. Define Sparkle Bureaucracy as the experiment lab prototype.
2. Use the agent-assisted diary and faculty feedback loop as the lab's operating infrastructure.
3. Use faculty feedback to choose and harden the first public/civic-facing experiment sequence.
4. Turn each candidate experiment into a comparable card before booking dates.

### What Would Change My Mind

- Faculty say an experiment lab is too broad unless one public/civic-facing experiment is clearly primary.
- A bounded community offers a concrete date for a liquid democracy or synthetic voice test.
- Prior art shows that one candidate is obsolete or predictably doomed.
- A mentor identifies a sharper civic community and user need for one option.

## Google Doc Feedback Surface

Primary faculty feedback should happen in a Google Doc, because faculty can comment inline and respond without navigating the repo. Use [faculty-feedback-brief.md](./faculty-feedback-brief.md) as the source text for that document.

After each round of comments:

1. Add a row to the feedback log below.
2. Update the relevant experiment card in [experiments.md](./experiments.md).
3. Add any new evidence requirement to [submission-diary.md](./submission-diary.md) or [engagement-log.md](./engagement-log.md).
4. Record unresolved questions here.

---

## Questions for Faculty

1. Does Sparkle Bureaucracy work as the assessed prototype if framed as an experiment lab?
2. Which first experiment best satisfies the political technology criteria?
3. Which candidate has the clearest civic community and evidence path?
3. Which candidate is most original relative to prior art?
4. Which candidate has the most serious methodological risk?
5. What would you need to see by the next check-in to believe this is on track?
6. Should the agent-assisted diary count as part of the experiment lab, or only as project infrastructure?
7. Where does the playfulness become ethically risky?
8. What kind of engagement evidence would be strongest: interviews, demos, usage logs, testimonials, workshop notes, or live event participation?
