# Prospective Sparkle Bureaucracy Experiments

This is the working idea bank for future Sparkle Bureaucracy experiments.

The earlier list was useful, but too many candidates read like generic civic workshops. This version uses the project source material more strictly: Sparkle Border Authority, Project Mirror, the PoliTech Awards process, Claw agents, Newspeak governance field notes, and the 80% credible institution / 20% sparkle principle.

## What Counts

A Sparkle Bureaucracy experiment should have all four parts:

1. **Institutional skeleton:** a recognisable bureaucratic ritual, such as a queue, permit, customs desk, warrant, court, audit, ledger, passport, hearing, register, or receipt.
2. **Affective re-skin:** the procedure feels participatory, playful, legible, or ceremonial without pretending power has disappeared.
3. **Decision pressure:** something is accepted, rejected, classified, authorised, appealed, represented, delegated, scored, or made contestable.
4. **Durable artifact:** the experiment leaves behind a receipt, ledger, passport, warrant, field kit, constitution, register, dashboard, or protocol that could harden into a reusable pattern.

If an idea does not change who can act, how judgment is made, what becomes visible, or how a decision can be contested, it is probably not strong enough yet.

## Selection Criteria

- Does it preserve a serious procedural skeleton?
- Does the sparkle layer change participation, not only aesthetics?
- What artifact survives the event?
- What evidence would show that the artifact mattered?
- What is the coercive or exclusionary part of the ritual, and is it still visible?
- Could this become Sparkle Bureaucracy's `pol.is`-like canonical tool or pattern?
- Could faculty critique the method, ethics, and political technology claim?
- Can it be run with fictional or synthetic cases before touching real people or sensitive data?

## 1. Public Voice Customs

**Working question:** How should public consultations handle civic voice when submissions may be human, AI-assisted, duplicated, synthetic, coordinated, affected, performative, or genuinely urgent?

**Bureaucratic ritual:** Public consultation, petitions, comment inboxes, casework triage, submission review.

**Sparkle move:** Re-skin "the public has spoken" as a customs process for civic voice. Every submission passes through counters that stamp different properties separately: volume, affectedness, duplication, AI assistance, uncertainty, evidence, and civic substance.

This must not become bot detection. The point is not to decide whether a comment is "real." The point is to preserve participation while making uncertainty legible.

**Format:** Mechanism plus live consultation experience.

**Smallest credible run:**

- Pick one low-risk Newspeak or community decision.
- Collect 20-40 submissions.
- Add labelled test cases: duplicates, AI-assisted genuine responses, synthetic objections, campaign text, translated or accessibility-assisted comments.
- Have reviewers process the set once as a normal inbox and once through Public Voice Customs.
- Publish a decision trace memo showing what changed.

**Artifacts left behind:** Public Voice Ledger, submission passports, uncertainty stamps, decision trace memo, "do not penalise accessibility assistance" rule.

**Evidence:** Reviewer confidence, false positives and negatives, minority-view preservation, whether the final decision cites themes that came through the process.

**Risks:** Surveillance creep, authenticity policing, punishing AI-assisted access needs, giving officials a language for discounting inconvenient participation.

**Canonical potential:** Very high. This could become "pol.is for synthetic-voice-era consultations" if the ledger is strong enough.

## 2. Mirror Commons

**Working question:** What does consentful synthetic representation look like when people manage their own evaluator agents?

**Bureaucratic ritual:** Jury selection, conflict-of-interest declarations, representative mandates, value constitutions, deliberation records.

**Sparkle move:** Turn Project Mirror's inferred constitutions into editable evaluator passports. An agent may only speak, rank, summarise, or deliberate inside the warrant a person has declared.

This is not "build a better clone." It is a consular process for deciding what a synthetic stand-in is allowed to do.

**Format:** AI mechanism plus constitutional calibration game.

**Smallest credible run:**

- Recruit 8 people.
- Each person creates or reviews an evaluator passport: criteria, modifiers, values, red lines, forbidden claims, expiry.
- Agents or manually simulated stand-ins rank 20 proposals.
- Participants audit where the stand-in misrepresented them.
- Run one aggregation comparison: Borda, pairwise, or average.

**Artifacts left behind:** Evaluator passports, modifier ledgers, dissent notes, agent-behaviour diff reports, aggregation receipts, revocation form.

**Evidence:** Human-agent agreement and disagreement, correction frequency, what participants refuse to delegate, comfort and discomfort notes, ranking stability after corrections.

**Risks:** False authority, flattened values, consent ambiguity, treating public records as permission, making synthetic judgment seem more precise than it is.

**Canonical potential:** Very high if Sparkle Bureaucracy becomes about managed synthetic selves for collective judgment.

## 3. The Exception Window

**Working question:** Can verification, eligibility, and queueing systems make rejection contestable without hiding exclusion or increasing surveillance?

**Bureaucratic ritual:** Queues, eligibility checks, identity verification, denial notices, appeals, exception handling.

**Sparkle move:** The exception route becomes the main object. Classification, rejection, override, and appeal are visible, named, receipted, and testable.

This is not a nicer DMV. It is a public service simulation where the question is whether the rejected person understands what happened and has a real route to challenge it.

**Format:** Live experience plus procedural mechanism.

**Smallest credible run:**

- Use 12 synthetic cases.
- Run a baseline queue, then a Sparkle queue.
- Include edge cases: name mismatch, no photo ID, expired documents, low digital access, accessibility need, synthetic media suspicion.
- Roles: applicant, queue marshal, verifier, exception officer, receipt clerk, observer.

**Artifacts left behind:** Queue tokens, classification cards, decision receipts, appeal stamps, exception ledger, service map.

**Evidence:** Participant comprehension, false reject and false accept handling, perceived dignity, whether the appeal route feels real, whether repeated exceptions reveal a broken rule, practitioner critique.

**Risks:** Cute-washing exclusion, decorative appeals, identity and accessibility harms, making a bad rule easier to endure instead of easier to contest.

**Canonical potential:** High as a portable pattern: decision receipt plus exception ledger.

## 4. Permission Passport Office

**Working question:** How can a rule-light community make permission to act explicit without reproducing hierarchy?

**Bureaucratic ritual:** Mandate seeking, role assignment, authorisation, sponsor letters, expiry, revocation.

**Sparkle move:** Make Newspeak's shadow permission structure visible through temporary warrants, stamps, sponsors, expiry dates, scope limits, and revocation slips.

The point is not to help people "feel confident." The point is to reveal unequal permission capacity: who can act without asking, who needs a document, and who silently carries legitimacy work.

**Format:** Governance mechanism plus role-played office.

**Smallest credible run:**

- 6-10 participants bring 3 blocked actions.
- The office issues, denies, narrows, or amends mandates.
- Every mandate names what it unlocks: room, budget, mailing list, calendar slot, role, or decision right.
- Review after one week.

**Artifacts left behind:** Permission passports, mandate registry, role charters, revocation slips, refusal reasons.

**Evidence:** Actions unblocked, contestations raised, who felt more authorised, whether mandates survived the week, whether the process reduced invisible negotiation or added another gate.

**Risks:** Formalising hierarchy, permission theatre, strengthening already-powerful actors, turning structural permission inequality into a personal confidence problem.

**Canonical potential:** Medium-high. This could become the permission scaffold pattern.

## 5. The Value Court

**Working question:** Can a group make evaluative values explicit before using them to judge people, projects, grants, tenders, or proposals?

**Bureaucratic ritual:** Awards judging, grant panels, procurement scoring, conflict declarations, appeals, method changes.

**Sparkle move:** Turn the PoliTech Awards process into public theatre: every scoring system is a constitution, every version change is a political act, every winner gets a stability receipt.

The experiment does not pretend to make evaluation neutral. It makes the trade-offs discussable.

**Format:** Game plus evaluation mechanism.

**Smallest credible run:**

- 8 jurors.
- 12 projects or proposals.
- 2-3 scoring constitutions.
- Compare Borda, pairwise, and average aggregation.
- Hold one objection round and one method-change round.

**Artifacts left behind:** Value constitutions, score passports, method-change receipts, winner-stability report, dissent appendix.

**Evidence:** Ranking volatility across methods, objections and amendments, participant understanding of value trade-offs, whether people accept an unwanted winner because the method is legible.

**Risks:** Method theatre, reputational harm if real people or projects are judged, synthetic judgment seeming more authoritative than it is, too much process weight for a small decision.

**Canonical potential:** Medium-high as an evaluation constitution kit.

## 6. Claw Civil Service

**Working question:** What happens when AI agents are treated as tiny civil servants with warrants, duties, refusal rules, audit logs, and decommission notices?

**Bureaucratic ritual:** Civil-service staffing, job descriptions, task dockets, warrants, supervision, audit logs, error reports.

**Sparkle move:** Claw-style agents become named officeholders, not magic helpers. Each agent has a job card, jurisdiction, source duties, refusal conditions, escalation route, and retirement procedure.

This is the lab's AI-native organisational futures experiment.

**Format:** Agent mechanism plus lab operating system.

**Smallest credible run:**

- Run 3 agents for 10 days:
  - Lab Clerk: classifies notes and drafts weekly diary updates.
  - Prior-Art Scout: finds adjacent work and flags confidence.
  - Skeptical Reviewer: challenges overclaims, ethics, and generic ideas.
- Every output is human-approved before publication.
- Keep rejected outputs.

**Artifacts left behind:** Agent warrant cards, task docket, source audit, error log, rejected-output file, decommission notice.

**Evidence:** Time saved, errors caught, outputs accepted, edited, or rejected, whether Fatima's voice is preserved, whether the warrant prevented scope creep.

**Risks:** Too meta for faculty, privacy leakage, anthropomorphic authority, flattening field notes or making the diary feel generated.

**Canonical potential:** High inside the lab. Lower as the public-facing canonical tool unless paired with Mirror Commons.

## 7. Shadow Labour Claims Office

**Working question:** How can invisible governance labour become visible, claimable, and redistributable without punishing the people who made it visible?

**Bureaucratic ritual:** Timesheets, expense claims, minutes, service credit, role registers, rota handoff.

**Sparkle move:** Translation, lobbying, emotional buffering, context carrying, access brokering, and legitimacy repair become claimable receipts. Shadow governance becomes inspectable without pretending it is neutral.

**Format:** Mechanism plus claims ceremony.

**Smallest credible run:**

- Use fictional cases first.
- Later, after one event or project week, 8-12 trusted participants file shadow-labour receipts.
- The group decides what becomes a named role, rotation, budget line, refusal, or stopped task.
- Review after two weeks.

**Artifacts left behind:** Shadow-labour receipts, invisible org chart, role-debt ledger, denied-claim reasons, transfer docket.

**Evidence:** Hidden work surfaced, redistribution decisions, role clarity, follow-through after two weeks, whether the people doing the work feel more or less burdened.

**Risks:** Making care transactional, social exposure, burdening already-burdened people, turning vulnerability into performance.

**Canonical potential:** Medium. More pattern than platform, but very specific to the governance field notes.

## 8. Liquid Mandate Assembly

**Working question:** How do paper, digital delegation, revocation, and visible mandate concentration affect legitimacy in a bounded community decision?

**Bureaucratic ritual:** Voting, delegation, revocation, ballot counting, audit notes, mandate registers.

**Sparkle move:** Delegation becomes tangible: mandate cards, revocation stamps, witnessed counts, paper overrides, concentration charts.

The point is not to solve voting. The point is to make legitimacy under visible power concentration observable.

**Format:** Governance experience plus audit mechanism.

**Smallest credible run:**

- 20-40 people decide a bounded real question: first experiment, small event budget, event priority, or community norm.
- Participants can vote directly or delegate.
- Paper ballot can confirm or override digital delegation.
- Publish an audit note.

**Artifacts left behind:** Delegation passports, sealed paper ballots, override receipts, concentration chart, audit note, dispute log.

**Evidence:** Direct vs delegated votes, revocations, tally mismatches, participant comprehension, willingness to accept a disliked outcome, discomfort around visible power concentration.

**Risks:** Clique power, secrecy leaks, social pressure, inflated legitimacy.

**Canonical potential:** Medium. Memorable, but riskier than Public Voice Customs or Mirror Commons.

## 9. Sparkle Border Field Kit

**Working question:** Can Sparkle Border Authority become a portable grammar for transforming access control, credentialing, and event entry into participatory ritual?

**Bureaucratic ritual:** Border crossing, access control, event check-in, credentialing, permit issuance, checkpoint override.

**Sparkle move:** Package the original Sparkle Border Authority pattern as a reusable field kit: intake, screening, decision, artifact, checkpoint, override.

This is the origin proof. The question is whether it travels.

**Format:** Deployable experience kit.

**Smallest credible run:**

- One event with 30+ entrants.
- Two checkpoint staff.
- Live override handling.
- Printed credentials.
- Dashboard screenshots.

**Artifacts left behind:** Visa stickers, checkpoint ledger, override report, rejection narratives, dashboard screenshots, host setup guide.

**Evidence:** Participation rate, override demand, emotional response to gating, artifact retention, accessibility issues, co-created lore.

**Risks:** Trivialising border violence, accessibility failures, exclusion through play, becoming the only thing people think Sparkle Bureaucracy is.

**Canonical potential:** High as brand origin and demo. Lower as the wider civic canonical tool unless translated into less border-specific contexts.

## Current Recommendation

There are two credible routes.

### Route A: Canonical Tool Route

If Sparkle Bureaucracy needs its `pol.is`-like anchor, prioritise:

1. Public Voice Customs.
2. Mirror Commons.
3. The Exception Window.

These have the strongest chance of becoming portable tools or protocols.

### Route B: Fellowship Evidence Route

If the goal is a strong, feasible fellowship prototype in the next few weeks, prioritise:

1. The Exception Window.
2. Claw Civil Service.
3. Permission Passport Office.

These best match existing assets, can be run with synthetic cases, and can produce assessable evidence quickly.

### Suggested First Run

Run **The Exception Window** as the first public-facing experiment:

- It inherits the Sparkle Border Authority grammar.
- It keeps enforcement visible.
- It produces durable artifacts: decision receipts and exception ledgers.
- It can be tested with fake cases.
- It lets faculty critique the ethics of making exclusion feel humane.

Run **Claw Civil Service** quietly in parallel as lab infrastructure:

- It keeps the diary moving.
- It tests AI-native organisational futures.
- It produces an error log and warrant system.
- It should not be the only public-facing prototype.

Keep **Public Voice Customs** and **Mirror Commons** as the bigger canonical candidates for the next phase.
